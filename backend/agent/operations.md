# kino edit operations

The complete reference for the operations an agent sends with the `edit` tool. Agents read it one topic at a time with `library` (`{topic:"op", name}`, `{topic:"rules", name}` or `{topic:"search", search}`), so the tool list stays small. People can read the whole file. `edit` also accepts `op` for `type`, names and short codes for IDs, and fills in `sceneId` when it is clear.

Each `## topic` below names the operations it covers on its `ops:` line. The text is the rule as the engine enforces it; `backend/agent-replies.test.mjs` checks that every operation type is covered and that no topic sends agents to controls of the removed manual editor.

## operations: Every operation and its fields
ops: -

Every operation is an object with `type` plus the fields below. Every layer or animation operation also needs `sceneId`. IDs are yours to choose when you create something: pass `properties.id` to add_layer and add_scene, `id` (or `animation.id`) to add_animation, `groupId` to group_layers and create_mask (letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `:`, `-`, at most 128 characters, unique in the project). An edit result lists the IDs it `created`, `changed` and `removed`, the `times` of at most 8 animations it made or retimed (`timesMore` counts the rest), and in `fixed` only what kino corrected (a color word, a role one letter off); the short field names (`layer`, `layers`, `scene`, `parent`, `component`, `animation`) are not listed. `dry_run: true` returns the same result and saves nothing.

A refused edit saves nothing. Its answer has the `reason`, the failing op's number and `type`, `rules` (the op's fields on one line) and `example`: one op that works, with the layer you named when it fits (copy it and change the values). `edit {retry, fix}` resends the batch with your fixes. A shape agents often send that can only mean one thing is changed and listed in `fixed` instead: an op name agents guess (add_text, animate, remove_layer, move_layer), `type` or a nested `layer: {type}` for add_layer, CSS units, percents, colours, gradients and cubic-bezier(), `font`, `color` and `content` for fontFamily, fill and text, a property preset sent with values (`preset: "move", parameters: {x}`) or an animation with from/to and no kind (both become a custom animation; from left out starts where the layer is drawn at its start), seconds as text ("500ms"), a clip speed as text ("0.5x"), `end` or `delay`, fields sent beside `properties`, one `layer` on an op that takes `layers`, a delete_layer with a list, and `ops` sent as one object. A percent x or y, keyframes and a file path as an image's `src` are refused with the way that works. `backend/agent/common-mistakes.mjs` has every case and `backend/agent-common-mistakes.test.mjs` runs them.

## requests: Revisions, retries and request IDs
ops: -

Apply validated scene, layer, and animation operations to a saved project. Supply the revision read from the project. If it is stale, ID-addressed operations are replayed against the latest project state only when their targets and assumptions still validate; obsolete or invalid targets return a revision conflict, so not every stale request succeeds. A successful replay returns rebased:true. Optional requestId (1 to 128 letters, digits, underscores, periods, colons, or hyphens) makes a request idempotent within its project and authorization scope (owner or share grant, not actor). The exact result is retained for the last 100 IDs per project and scope. Reusing the same scoped ID with a different revision or operations payload is rejected. Every call's operations are saved together as one revision, and nothing is saved when any of them is refused.

## presets: Catalog animation presets (In/Out)
ops: add_animation, update_animation, update_animations

Add editable catalog presets with add_animation preset: preset-appear, preset-fade, preset-slide, preset-mask, preset-grow, preset-shrink, preset-blur, preset-blur-slide, preset-wipe, or preset-blink. Legacy IDs fade-in/fade-out, slide-up/down/left/right, grow-in/shrink-out, blur-in/blur-out and rotate-in are accepted. Custom blur remains the Layer Blur action. Optional mode is in or out; parameters depend on the preset: slide {direction,distance}, mask {direction}, grow/shrink {scale}, blur {blur}, blur-slide {direction,distance,blur}, wipe {direction,softness}, blink {count}; wipe and mask (not on text) also take the wipe settings below; appear/fade take no parameters. Directions are up/down/left/right. An exit reverses its entrance: a slide out with direction up leaves downward (its name says Slide Out ↓), so to leave upward use direction down; a wipe or mask out hides toward where its entrance started. An exit saved before 2026-10-20 may still show the direction's arrow (Slide Out ↑ for direction up): its from and to show the way it moves, and a preset change gives it the new name. Distance and scale are percentages from 0 to 1000, blur is pixels from 0 to 1000, softness is 0 to 100 percent, and count is an integer from 1 to 20. Add accepts duration, easing and intensity overrides; without one a preset is smooth both ways, appear, fade and blink are linear, a whip arrives expo-out and leaves ease-in (effect presets keep their own), and a mode change on a default easing takes the new mode's default. Default lengths: fade 0.5 s, grow/shrink 0.7 s, rotate 1.05 s, other presets 0.8 s; custom move 1 s, scale 0.8 s, opacity 0.5 s. Stored preset metadata is {id,mode,parameters}, using base ids without preset-. update_animation or update_animations properties may supply a partial preset such as {preset:{mode:out,parameters:{distance:75}}} (mode, parameters, or one of the preset's own settings such as direction sent beside the other properties goes into preset the same way); values regenerate from the current design state, retaining timing/easing and row identity. A preset change works out kind, from and to itself, so one sent with from, to, discrete, motionPath, anchor, fromCurrent or a kind other than in or out is refused (they would be lost) and nothing is saved: the refusal gives the op with the preset change alone and the op with those values alone. A kind of in or out beside a preset change is read as its mode, and refused when it disagrees with the mode sent. A setting or mode sent both beside the preset and in it (or in parameters) with two values is refused, naming both. Editing from/to/kind directly detaches preset metadata, so a later partial preset is refused: give the id to make it a preset again ({preset:{id:"slide",mode:"in",parameters:{direction:"up"}}}). A custom animation, or one named like no preset, never had a preset: its refusal gives the op that changes its from and to instead. preset:null also detaches it without changing values. When a layer moves, the receipt sends a preset again ({preset:{id,parameters}}) rather than its from/to when that gives the same places. Timing/easing edits keep metadata. Legacy endpoint-only documents are never regenerated on load. Mask/Wipe endpoints use reveal:{progress:0..1,direction:up/down/left/right,softness:0..100}; missing reveal means fully shown. Wipe settings (optional on reveal and on the wipe and mask presets; left out, the wipe is the straight one of direction): shape linear (default) | radial (an oval in the layer's proportions grows from the center) | iris (a circle grows from the center) | clock (a hand sweeps around the center, clockwise); angle -3600..3600 degrees, for linear the way the edge moves (0 right, 90 down, 180 left, 270 up; default from direction) and for clock where the hand starts (default -90, 12 o'clock); centerX, centerY -1..2 as fractions of the layer's box (default 0.5); edgeWidth 0..10000 (default 0) puts a band of edgeColor (hex, default #FFFFFF) on the layer's own drawing just ahead of the picture, px wide (degrees for clock), solid with its front soft over the softness. invert true (reveal only) shows the side the edge has not reached at 1 - progress, with no band. Lottie export refuses a wipe with any of these settings. Appear uses discrete:{type:instant} on opacity; Blink uses discrete:{type:blink,count:1..20}. Both respect In pre-start and Out final-state holding.

## easing: Easing and intensity
ops: add_animation, update_animation, update_animations

Timed animations (including Counter) accept easing and optional intensity both on a full animation and as add_animation preset overrides; nested Change text/Counter effects also accept intensity. Intensity is an integer from 0 to 100 (default 50), with no effect for None or Custom easing. Easing accepts canonical IDs and the older/Pro labels None, Slow down, and Accelerate, which are stored canonically. Fixed named curves (intensity does not change them): expo-out [0.16,1,0.3,1] (fast in, long settle; "snap" reaches it), quart-out [0.25,1,0.5,1] (softer), in-out [0.65,0,0.35,1]; easeOutExpo, ease-in-out and similar spellings reach them. smooth at intensity 50 is [0.5,0,0,1] and natural [0.8,0,0.2,1]. spring (a damped bounce that settles exactly on 1) and back-out (one overshoot; easeOutBack reaches it): intensity sets their bounce and overshoot. steps holds poses (stop-motion): intensity is the count (1 to 100, 0 counts as 1), the first pose the from value and the last the to value, landing at the end; easing "steps-4" sends both, and a bare "steps" without intensity is refused. A key's ease steps counts by its animation's intensity; a move's ease takes "steps-4" too. Lottie holds each jump on transform and opacity. Change text and Counter effects, and full animations of kind in or out added without an easing, default to smooth. Top-level Hide, Show, and Change text operations refuse intensity. The saved easing library stores copied values, is workspace-wide and owner-only, and has no project or account coupling.

## motion-path: Curved Move paths
ops: add_animation, update_animation

A custom Move whose from/to contain only x and/or y accepts motionPath on the animation, as an add_animation override, or in update_animation properties. Use {points:[{x,y,in:{x,y},out:{x,y}}],bezierControls:true,alignToPath:false}; at most 64 intermediate points, finite coordinates and handle offsets from -1000000 to 1000000. Points use the endpoints' parent-space box coordinates, handles are relative offsets, omitted handles are zero, and omitted flags default as shown. Missing endpoint axes use the layer's design x/y. Curved Moves own both axes, travel by arc length with the chosen easing, and optionally add the tangent angle to rotation, retaining it after the Move ends. An empty points list keeps ordinary straight Move behavior; null removes the stored path. alignmentOffset is an optional finite rotation compensation in degrees (-360000 to 360000), maintained by reparenting; leave it omitted for new paths. Other animation kinds and mixed-property tracks refuse motionPath.

## scene-background: Scene background, canvas position and duplication
ops: add_scene, update_scene, duplicate_scene, insert_component, stagger_animations

add_scene and update_scene properties may set background to a hex color or a gradient {type: linear or radial, angle, stops: [{offset: 0..1, color: hex}, ...]} with 2 to 64 ordered stops, backgroundEnabled to true or false, and backgroundAnimations to a complete list of Color animations. A background animation has a document-unique id, name, kind in/out/custom, start, duration, easing, from/to containing only fill, and the normal timed-animation options; Move, Hide, Show and other non-Color background operations are refused. Fill off retains the paint and animations without drawing them. Background animations use update_scene; they are excluded from stagger_animations and are not stored layers. An image or video canvas background is ordinary content: a locked layer named Background, parentId null, at index 0 (bottom). Images use the scene box and cover cropping; videos use a centered box scaled to cover the scene while preserving source proportions. Use uploaded media src through the usual layer operations; transparent exports retain this media content. Component instances accept componentBackgroundEnabled (boolean, default true). False suppresses only the linked source scene's background fill and Color animations; its content stays linked. Scene duplication uses false on its linked instance because the copied scene owns its background paint and animations. Artboard canvasX and canvasY are finite top-left presentation coordinates from -100000000 to 100000000; update_scene can set them without changing layer coordinates or exports. aspectLocked (true or false) is the scene's stored aspect-ratio lock; update_scene still sets width and height exactly as given, and drawing and exports ignore it. New scenes and duplicate_scene copies are placed to the right of the rightmost artboard. duplicate_scene takes sceneId, duplicates layers and animations with new IDs, and preserves the linked-copy behavior of a main component.

## replace-media: Replacing a layer's media
ops: replace_media

replace_media (sceneId, layerId, mediaId including its extension, returned by import_media) replaces an uploaded source on the same layer ID. Images, GIFs and videos may replace each other; audio only replaces audio. It preserves the layer box, transform, opacity, effects, comments and animations, updates the filename only if it was still the old file's name, and clamps Play offsets and duration to the new source. A still removes Play; a new timed source gets normal import timing. Missing, unreadable, unsupported and inaccessible media are refused; view links cannot edit. Changing an image master to video is refused while ordinary, nested or Bulk Create instances have image source overrides; reset or detach those instances first. GET /api/media/<mediaId>/metadata returns the original name, byte size and source facts; GET /api/media/<mediaId>/download saves original bytes with viewing permission.

## bulk-create: Bulk Create from CSV
ops: bulk_create

bulk_create (sceneId, exactly one of csv or rows with the header first, optional deleteMissing) creates or updates named component variants from text and image cells. Image cells accept permitted saved image filenames or /api/media sources returned by import_media. Missing variants are only deleted when deleteMissing is true, and that edit first asks the person to approve deleting those scenes (by name); a dry_run never asks. If the allowed edit would then delete a scene the approval did not name, it deletes nothing and asks again.

## animation-groups: Composed animation groups
ops: update_animation, update_animations, delete_animation, rename_animation, group_animations, duplicate_animations, convert_to_actions, ungroup_animations

Animation IDs address either a top-level animation or a member inside Composed groups. update_animation and update_animations take absolute scene times for nested members; rename_animation and delete_animation target nested IDs directly, and deleting the final member also removes its now-empty group. group_animations needs at least two sibling animations with the same owner and parent, and optional name labels the Composed row. The group keeps member timing relative to its start; its full playback duration is derived from its members. update_animation or update_animations can change a Composed group's name or duration, and a duration edit proportionally scales its members and their text delays. duplicate_animations copies selected rows at the same time with fresh IDs, including fresh IDs for every copied group member. convert_to_actions accepts selected, supported non-text preset animations and replaces each with a Composed group of independent property actions at the same start and timing; text, custom, or unsupported presets are refused.

## align: Aligning layers
ops: align_layers

align_layers (sceneId, layerIds, align: "left", "center", "right", "top", "middle", or "bottom"; center and middle are the horizontal and vertical centers). align_layers lines up one layer with its parent group, or with the scene when it is not in a group, and lines up several layers with the box around all of them. It uses each layer's on-canvas box after rotation, scale, and parent groups (auto-sized text is measured the way it is drawn), changes only x and y, and shifts animated x and y values by the same amount so motion keeps its shape.

## group-layers: Grouping and ungrouping layers
ops: group_layers, ungroup_layer

group_layers (sceneId, layerIds, optional name, optional groupId) puts layers into a new group. One layer is enough, and the layers may come from different groups. The group goes into the parent of the selected layer drawn on top and takes its place in the stacking order; the layers keep their order inside it, and a selected layer whose group is also selected moves with that group. The group's box is the box around the layers after rotation, and every layer keeps its on-canvas look at every time: x, y, rotation, scale, skew, and their animated values change to match the new parent. Pass groupId to choose the new group's ID; otherwise look the group up by name in the returned project. ungroup_layer (sceneId, layerId) removes a group and bakes its move, rotation, and scale into its layers, which take the group's place in the stacking order. Both refuse with an explanation instead of changing how layers look: for example a group with its own animations or appearance effects (opacity, blur, shadow, blend mode, mask), a mask separated from the layer it masks, a rotation animation inside an unevenly stretched group, or layers drawn from a component instance (edit the main component or detach the instance).

## extract-component: Making a component from layers
ops: create_component_from_layers, update_scene

create_component_from_layers (sceneId, layerIds, optional name, optional componentSceneId, optional instanceId) groups the selection with those same appearance and mask checks, moves its contents into a new transparent main component directly after the source scene, and leaves a linked instance in the group's place and parent. The component's dimensions are the grouped width and height rounded up to at least one pixel, its duration is the source scene's duration, and layer and animation IDs are kept. The instance starts at 0, has componentVisibility "both" and clipContent false so outside strokes, shadows and blur still draw. Open-ended video and audio are pinned through the source scene end. Extracted components save componentPlayback "scene" so their source clock continues for at least the scene duration, preserving finite video/GIF Play visibility and Freeze settings after Play ends. Other components default to componentPlayback "content"; update_scene can set either mode. Without name, one selected layer supplies its name; several use "Component <n>", where n is the number of component scenes plus one. Scene and instance share that name. Empty or inherited selections, unsafe grouping, scene limits and component nesting beyond 16 levels are refused without saving any change. When extracting inside an existing main component, instance override paths follow the moved layers and their position overrides use the new origin. A selection across different parents is refused if an existing instance's overrides could change its appearance.

## masks: Masks
ops: create_mask, remove_mask, group_layers, ungroup_layer

create_mask (sceneId, layerIds with at least two layers, optional maskLayerId, optional name, optional groupId, optional maskInvert, optional maskMode) groups the layers the same way as group_layers into a new mask group (isMaskGroup true, default name "Mask group") and makes the bottom-most of them, or maskLayerId, the mask (isMask true) at the bottom of that group. A maskLayerId that is not the bottom-most layer moves to the bottom of the group, and it stays there after the mask is removed. A mask is a luminance mask: the masked layers show where the mask is drawn white and are hidden where it is black or empty. In a mask group the mask covers every visible layer above it in the group, including layers inside nested groups; elsewhere a mask covers only the next visible layer above it. maskInvert true (default false) swaps inside and outside: the layers show everywhere except where the mask is. maskMode "alpha" (default "luminance") cuts by the mask's see-through parts instead of its brightness, so a picture logo with transparency cuts its own shape. Set both on the mask group (update_layer, or in create_mask) or on the mask layer; the group's value wins. Feather is the mask layer's blur and strength its opacity. Lottie export maps them to matching track mattes. Grouping refuses to move a layer from one side of a mask group's mask to the other. remove_mask (sceneId, layerId of a mask group) ungroups the mask group when that keeps every layer's look, so the mask becomes an ordinary layer again and every layer keeps its place on the canvas. When the group can't be ungrouped that way (for example it is animated, faded, blurred, or clips its layers), remove_mask keeps the group and sets its isMaskGroup and its mask's isMask to false. ungroup_layer on a mask group also removes the mask, but refuses such groups.

## layer-order: Layer order and moving layers between groups
ops: reorder_layer, move_layers

reorder_layer (sceneId, layerId, to: "front", "back", "forward", "backward", or a whole number) changes a layer's place among the layers of its own group (front is the top, back the bottom, forward and backward one place); a number is its place counted from 0 at the bottom. move_layers (sceneId, layerIds, and position "above" or "below" with referenceId, or position "top" or "bottom" with parentId, which is null or left out for the scene's top level) moves layers in the layer order, also into and out of groups. A listed layer whose group is also listed moves with that group, the moved layers keep their order, and each moved layer keeps its place, size, and turn on the canvas (x, y, rotation, scale, and their animated values change to match the new group), while it takes on the new group's own effects such as opacity, masking, and clipping. move_layers refuses moving a layer into or out of a group whose place, size, turn, scale, or skew is animated, because the layer could not keep its place while that group moves. In a mask group the mask stays below the layers it masks and no layer crosses it: reorder_layer's front, back, forward, and backward keep a layer on its own side of the mask, the mask itself can't be moved up, and a layer moved into a mask group must be placed above its mask. reorder_layer and move_layers refuse moving a group into itself, moving layers into a component instance, moving a layer from one side of a mask group's mask to the other, and changes that would make a mask mask a different layer or give a mask group a different mask. A mask that has nothing to mask yet (the top layer of its group) starts masking a layer moved just above it only with move_layers mask: true; without it the move is refused, so no move masks a layer by accident.

## layer-flags: Hiding (visible:false), locking and renaming layers
ops: set_layer_flags, update_layer

To hide one layer, update_layer with properties {"visible":false} (true shows it again); it stays in the project with its animations, and a hidden layer is never drawn, whatever its animations do. opacity 0 does not hide a layer whose fade-in or other animation sets opacity: the animation's value is drawn over the layer's, and a fade-out that starts from opacity 1 brings it back when it starts (the edit's receipt warns and gives the update_animation to send). An animation's x and y are places in the scene, so update_layer x or y leaves them where they were; the receipt then gives update_animation ops that move every animation of that place by the layer's own move (new value minus old), both ends, so a slide keeps its direction and a chain of moves stays joined. set_layer_flags (sceneId, layerIds, and visible, locked, or both: true or false) hides, shows, locks or unlocks layers. A hidden group hides every layer inside it and a locked group locks every layer inside it, without changing their own flags. It refuses hiding or showing that would leave a mask with no visible layer above it to mask. Rename a layer with update_layer and properties.name.

## text-ranges: Styling part of a text layer
ops: style_text_range, update_layer

Text layers accept textStyleRanges: at most 2048 sorted, non-overlapping {start,end,style} entries, with raw UTF-16 offsets on grapheme boundaries. Style supports fontFamily, fontSource, fontWeight, fontStyle, fontSize, fill and letterSpacing; sources must be safe local font URLs. style_text_range (sceneId, layerId, start/end or first-match text, style) changes only supplied fields in one range. Replacing text through update_layer without explicit ranges clears previous ranges. Whole-layer style edits clear only matching range fields. When the layer's font lacks a character (such as "∞"), a range over just that character with fontFamily, fontWeight and fontSource of a font that has it draws it (library {topic:"fonts", search:"∞"} lists such fonts; kino downloads the face after the edit).

## path-fill-rule: Path fill rule
ops: update_layer, update_layers

Path layers have fillRule "nonzero" (the default for old documents) or "evenodd". Set it with update_layer, or update_layers when every listed layer is a path; other layer types and values are refused. The rule applies to the whole path and stays in effect while path morph animations change its geometry.

## layer-style: Fill, stroke, paints and gradients
ops: add_layer, update_layer, update_layers, update_animation

lockAspect (true or false) is the layer's aspect-ratio lock: a multi_transform scale that includes such a layer needs equal width and height scales. update_layer and update_layers still set width and height exactly as given, and drawing and exports ignore lockAspect. fillEnabled (true or false, default true) turns the fill on or off on rectangle, ellipse, star, path, and text layers: with false, `look` and every export (PNG, GIF, video, and Lottie) draw the layer with no fill, and the layer keeps its fill paint, so setting fillEnabled back to true shows the same color or gradient. While fillEnabled is false, an animation of fill still changes the stored paint but draws nothing; the stroke is still drawn. A layer's stroke is off when strokeWidth is 0. A paint (fill, stroke, and the from and to values of a fill or stroke animation) is a hex color (#rgb, #rgba, #rrggbb, or #rrggbbaa) or a gradient {type: "linear" or "radial", angle in degrees (only a linear gradient draws it), stops: 2 to 64 {offset from 0 to 1 in order, color as hex, optional id unique within the paint (non-empty string up to 256 characters)}}; anything else is refused. Linear angles are clockwise from left-to-right; the line through the local layer-box center meets its edges (45 degrees reaches a square's corners, 90 degrees points down). Rotating, flipping or evenly spacing a gradient needs no special operation: update_layer fill/stroke, or update_animation from/to, can set the resulting angle and ordered stops. Preserve stop IDs when moving stops. Radial paints have no rendered handle direction to rotate.

Stacked strokes: a text, rectangle, ellipse, star or path layer's optional strokes is a list of up to 16 {color hex, width 0..10000 px, align? center|inside|outside (center), opacity? 0..1 (1), dash? [on, off, ...] px (solid), join? miter|round|bevel (miter)}, drawn after the single stroke, later ones over earlier ones. On text it outlines all the letters together, and outside strokes listed first stay under every letter also when letters or words animate apart or after outline_text; on shapes it follows strokeStart, strokeEnd and strokeOffset. Sticker: wide outside outlines first, round join. Neon: fillEnabled false, one color wide and faint to a thin bright core. The list does not animate; [] or null removes it. Lottie export refuses it.

## color-animation: Color animations and how paints mix
ops: add_animation, update_animation, add_scene, update_scene, insert_component, add_layer, update_layer

add_animation with preset "color" adds a Color animation (rectangle, ellipse, star, path, text, and group layers only): from.fill is the layer's current fill and to.fill a contrasting paint of the same kind, and update_animation can set either one to any paint. An animation with fill in from or to is refused on any other layer type (image, video, audio, component), whether it comes from add_animation, add_layer, update_layer, insert_component, or the layers of add_scene and update_scene, and so is an update_animation(s) that changes its fill values. A document saved with one on such a layer (older versions accepted it) still opens and can be edited and exported; it draws nothing, and its name, timing, and easing can still change. Between from and to the paint mixes as follows. Two colors mix red, green, blue, and alpha in a straight line. A color and a gradient mix as a gradient with the gradient's type, angle, and stop positions whose stops all start (or end) at that color. Two gradients with the same number of stops mix stop by stop, so stop positions, colors, and the angle move. With different stop counts, both gradients are first read at every stop position either one has, and then only the colors mix. Linear and radial switch type halfway through the eased progress and keep the linear gradient's angle. A Color animation never turns a disabled Fill on. `look` and the PNG, GIF, video, and PNG sequence exports draw the same paint; Lottie exports a native gradient with one stop count for every frame, and a switch between linear and radial becomes two gradients that swap with hold keyframes.

## color: Color grading (curves, LUTs, looks) and the add blend
ops: add_animation, update_animation, update_layer, match_color, color_filter

color_filter {layer | layers, filter, strength?} puts a named look on video, image, shape, text or group layers in one op: warm (white balance +22, saturation 1.08), cool (white balance -20, saturation 0.95), film (lifted, slightly teal blacks, warm whites, saturation 0.88), teal-orange, faded (lift 0.07, gain 0.94, saturation 0.75), vivid (saturation 1.3, a gentle S curve), mono (black and white with a gentle S curve) or bleach-bypass. Each is one or two ordinary Color effects named "Filter <name>" (and "Filter <name> 2"), covering the whole scene; strength 0 to 1 (default 1) moves every setting toward neutral. A second color_filter on a layer replaces its filter, and filter "none" takes it off; other grades on the layer stay.

match_color {layer | layers, to, at?} grades footage (video or image layers) so its color matches the reference layer `to`: mixed sources (a screen recording, stock, a phone walk) read as one grade. kino reads 3 small frames of each layer (and of `to`, or its one frame at scene time `at`), measures shadows, midtones, highlights and saturation in Oklab, and solves the grade with skin tones kept within 8 degrees of hue. It adds two ordinary effects to each layer: color-wheels named "Color match" and hue-saturation named "Color match saturation"; running it again replaces them. It reads the files' own pixels, so another grade on the layer or on `to` is not counted; files over 2048 px are refused (import a 1080p copy). The receipt's color gives each layer's parameters and the color distance before and after.

The Color effects grade a layer's colors: curves, lut, hue-saturation, white-balance, color-wheels, keep-color, color-ranges and color-look. Add one with add_animation {layer, effect, parameters}. Like every ranged effect it covers the whole scene unless you give start/duration, and several stack in the order added (each grades the result of the one before). They work on any visual layer; to grade several layers at once, put them in a group (group_layers) and grade the group. Each animates from `from` to its parameters: numbers and colors blend, two curves with the same number of points move point by point, and other values (a curve with another point count, lut, colorize) switch halfway.

Color space: every Color effect works on the sRGB values a frame stores, from 0 to 1 (display-referred, the space all kino effects use), and clips at 0 and 1; there is no HDR or wide gamut. Brightness ("luma") is Rec.709: 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B. White balance alone converts to linear light, multiplies, and converts back. A LUT is applied as written, so a LUT made for log footage expects log pictures.

- curves: master, red, green and blue are lists of 2 to 16 [input, output] points from 0 to 1 with increasing inputs. A smooth curve goes through the points without overshooting them (monotone cubic) and stays flat past the first and last point. Each channel goes through its own curve, then master. strength 0 to 1 mixes with the original.
- lut: import a .cube file (Adobe Cube LUT 1.0: TITLE, DOMAIN_MIN/MAX, LUT_1D_SIZE up to 65,536 or LUT_3D_SIZE 2 to 65) with project {action:"import", file}; a file that breaks the format is refused with the line that is wrong. Set parameters.lut to the src it answers. Interpolation is trilinear (tetrahedral is not offered). A 1D LUT, or a 3D LUT that changes each channel on its own, is exact; any other 3D LUT is drawn on a grid of at most 17 points along green and blue, within about 4/255 of exact trilinear interpolation (1 to 2 on average), and makes renders slower (a 1080p video export took 2.3 times as long). A project keeps its LUT files from deletion while it uses them.
- hue-saturation: hue turns colours around the grey axis by degrees (120 makes red green, 360 is a full turn back to the start; between those, saturated colours come out a little darker than an HSL hue shift). saturation 0 is grey and 2 doubles it; lightness -1 to 1 mixes toward black or white. colorize gives every pixel tint's hue at its own brightness, before hue, saturation and lightness.
- white-balance: temperature -100 to 100 cools or warms (+100 is red ×1.41 and blue ×0.71 in linear light); tint -100 to 100 goes green or magenta. A neutral grey keeps its brightness.
- color-wheels: lift moves the blacks, gamma the mid-tones and gain the whites (out = (gain · (x + lift · (1 − x)))^(1/gamma)); liftRed, gammaBlue and the like add to the master value for one channel. lift 0, gamma 1 and gain 1 change nothing.
- keep-color: keeps colours within hueRange degrees of targetColor's hue (fading out over softness more degrees) and turns every other colour toward its grey luma by amount. Greys have no hue and are never kept. Hue is measured on the colour wheel where red is 0°, yellow 60° and green 120°; the soft edge is accurate to about 4%.
- color-ranges: <range>Hue -180 to 180 turns, <range>Saturation -1 (gray of the same luma) to 1 (up to double, never clipped) and <range>Lightness -1 (black) to 1 (white) change one hue range: red 0°, orange 30°, yellow 60°, green 120°, aqua 180°, blue 240°, purple 270°, magenta 300°; a hue between two blends both. Colors within about 7% of gray are left alone and the change is full from 25%, so grays stay gray. highlights and shadows -1 to 1 then lift or sink the bright or dark tones by luma (black and white stay put). All 0 changes nothing.
- color-look: look matte (lifted blacks, soft), ember (red shadows, amber highlights, dense), frost (bright, cold, muted), ink (near black and white), dusk (violet shadows, peach highlights), meadow (green shadows, yellow light), harbor (teal shadows, cream highlights, muted) or punch (no tint, more color and contrast); amount 0 (unchanged) to 1 (the look as made, default). Each look is a tone curve, a shadow and a highlight tint and a saturation, drawn as a generated LUT. It is a whole grade: put it on alone, or on a group.
- The add blend: update_layer {properties:{blendMode:"add"}} adds the layer's colour to what is below it, clipped at white (linear dodge; CSS plus-lighter). The other blend modes are normal, multiply, screen, overlay, darken, lighten, color-dodge, color-burn, hard-light, soft-light, difference, exclusion, hue, saturation, color and luminosity.

## blur: Blur and edges
ops: add_animation, update_animation

directional-blur, zoom-blur and spin-blur smear a layer as if it moved while a camera's shutter was open. directional-blur smears along a line: length in the layer's own pixels (a layer scaled 2× smears twice as far on screen), angle 0 is to the right and 90 is down; the smear is centred on the layer. zoom-blur smears away from a centre: each point is drawn from its place out to 1 + amount times its distance from the centre (amount 0.3 is 30% farther), so a picture that fills the frame still fills it. spin-blur smears around a centre, angle degrees in all (half each way). centerX and centerY are fractions of the layer's box (0.5 is the middle; -1 to 2 reaches outside it). They are value effects: add_animation with from and parameters animates one, and a later one of the same key starts where the earlier one is when it leaves out from, so a blur that rises and falls is two animations. A whip pan is a fast move with natural easing (fastest at the middle) plus a directional blur along it that peaks there. The smear has about one copy per pixel of travel, so it shows no steps or ghost copies at large values.

edge-fill, mirror and kaleidoscope are ranged effects (the whole scene unless given start and duration). edge-fill repeats the picture past its box, extend 0 to 3 layer sizes on every side: mode mirror flips every other copy so the picture carries on across each edge; tile repeats it. Use it so a shake, spin, zoom-out or blur never uncovers an empty edge. mirror reflects one side of a line through the centre onto the other (angle 0 is an upright line that keeps the left half; 90 a level line that keeps the top half). kaleidoscope repeats a wedge of 180/segments degrees from angle around the centre, every other copy mirrored; segments 2 to 32; animate angle to turn it. mirror and kaleidoscope stay inside the layer's picture: put edge-fill before them to fill the corners. Where two pieces meet, the lower piece reaches 1.5 px past the joint so no dark line shows; on a layer turned at an angle a faint hairline can still show at a joint.

focus-band is depth of field on a flat layer: a band stays sharp and the blur rises smoothly outside it to maxBlur (px, as the layer's blur property) over falloff. shape line is a band across the layer through centerX/centerY (fractions of its box), angle 0 level; width and falloff are fractions of the layer's size across the band. shape rect is a sharp box centred there, width and height fractions of the layer's width and height, falloff a fraction of its height. angle turns a line only; a rect stays square to the layer. The band is in the layer's own coordinates: on a tilted plane (rotateX/rotateY, the ui-plane move) it tilts with the plane, and on a group (a split_image picture) it runs across every piece. It is a value effect: a later focus-band without from moves the band from where the earlier one is, so moving centerY racks focus from one row to another or follows a typing line; give every setting the band keeps, or it returns to its default. It draws three copies of the layer (four past 24 px of blur as drawn), mixed by gradient shares with no hard edge: about 0.3 to 0.4 s more per 1080p frame for a phone-size picture on a tilted plane.

All seven work on any visual layer: footage, images, text, shapes, groups and component instances (sequence parts). To blur several layers as one picture, group them (group_layers) and put the effect on the group. Effects run in the order of their start, then the order they were added, each on the result of the one before: put edge-fill first, then the wiggle, move or blur. A zoom or spin blur draws one copy of the layer per sample, up to 256; over another zoom or spin blur the two share 256, and over a layer that already has a filter (a directional blur, glow, shadow or blur) it draws at most 16, which can show steps at large values. So add a directional blur after a zoom or spin blur, not before: it looks smoother and draws 6 to 12 times faster.

Cost: exports draw these effects with Chromium, like the dashboard. A 1920×1080 frame took about 33 to 50 ms with no effect, 200 ms with a 400 px directional blur, 260 ms with zoom-blur 0.4, 290 ms with spin-blur 30°, 410 ms with edge fill and spin blur, 800 ms for zoom or spin blur on a full-frame photo, 0.4 s for spin blur then directional blur, and 2.5 to 4.8 s for directional blur then spin blur. A blur at 0 (length, amount or angle) draws nothing extra.

Motion blur from movement is a render setting, not an effect: render options {shutterAngle: 0 to 360, motionSamples: 2 to 16} (library rules render). It blurs whatever moves (position, scale, rotation, a whip) with nothing to set per layer, but only final renders show it, not the dashboard or look. The blur effects show everywhere and their size is yours to choose, so use them for a stylised whip, zoom or spin, or when the preview must show the smear. add_sequence has whip and spin transitions built from them (topic sequences).

Lottie: a layer with any of these effects is exported as a sequence of pictures drawn by Chromium (3× by default) with a lottie-effect-rasterized warning naming it; the rest of the scene stays vector.

## light: Glow, light rays and lens flare
ops: add_animation, update_animation, delete_animation

The Light effects add light to a layer: glow (bloom, neon, halation), light-rays (god rays) and lens-flare. Add one with add_animation {layer, effect, parameters}. Like every ranged effect it covers the whole scene unless you give start/duration, several stack in the order added, and each animates from `from` to its parameters (numbers and colours blend; style and source switch halfway). They work on footage, images, text, shapes and groups; to light several layers as one picture, group them (group_layers) and put the effect on the group. The light is drawn as part of the layer, so layers above it cover it and a group with clipContent cuts it off at the group's box.

- glow: the layer's bright parts, blurred and added back on top (out = layer + strength × glow, clipped at white). Brightness is Rec.709 luma; threshold picks the parts brighter than it and softness widens the ramp above it (threshold 0 with softness 0 glows the whole layer: the neon look on a title). radius is the blur size in the layer's own pixels; passes 2 to 6 add blurs at 2×, 4×, … the radius that share the same light (deep glow: bright near the source with a long faint tail). color with colorMix tints the glow (1 is all color). A strength animation from a high value to 0 on a cue is a flash on a beat.
- light-rays: streaks of light radiating from centerX/centerY (fractions of the layer's box; outside 0..1 is off the layer). source bright streaks the layer's bright parts (threshold and softness as in glow); source behind puts a soft light of lightSize (its diameter as a fraction of the layer's longer side) behind the layer, so its shapes cast shadows between the rays (light behind a title). length 0 to 0.9 is how far a streak reaches, as a fraction of its distance from the centre; decay 0 to 1 is how fast it fades along the way. The rays add light to the layer and to what is under it, stop at the edge of the scene, and reach at most one layer size past the layer's box.
- lens-flare: the shapes a bright light makes in a lens. x and y place the light as fractions of the layer's box (outside 0..1 is off the layer), so a flare on a small layer (a sun, a lamp) with x 0.5 and y 0.5 follows that layer when it moves. style glint is a core, a halo and star spikes; anamorphic a long thin streak along angle with stretched ghosts; rings a row of coloured discs and rings on the line from the light through the middle of the scene. size scales the whole flare, color tints it (the ghosts take nearby hues), and strength above 1 makes the light parts larger and hotter. The flare spans the scene, adds light, lays its ghosts down as faint colour too so they show on a light picture, and fades out as the light leaves the scene (gone 12% of the scene's diagonal past the edge).
- Cost: measured in a 1080p MP4 export (2 s at 30 fps, a busy machine; a plain scene drawn the same way took 26 to 60 ms a frame): glow 53 to 71 ms a frame, lens-flare 85 to 121 ms, light-rays 134 to 201 ms (behind costs more than bright). The dashboard's live preview: while rays show in a playing scene on a stage larger than 1280×720 device pixels, it is drawn at 1280×720 and enlarged, and the stage label says "drawn at 1280×720". With the stage zoomed to fill the window on a 2× Mac screen, one rays layer then plays at 30 fps (about 6 at full size); two rays layers that fill the frame about 25, three about 17. The paused picture is the exact export frame, and exports are not changed.
- Reduced motion: for people who ask their system for reduced motion, the dashboard preview draws a Light animation shorter than 1 s calmly: left out when it shows only for that moment (a flash on a beat), held at its start or end values when keepStill holds that end. Exports always draw the real thing.

## particles: Particle overlays
ops: add_layer, add_animation, update_animation

The Generate effects (embers, sparks, snow, dust, rain, bokeh, fog, light-leaks) draw particles made inside kino, so no stock overlay file is needed. Each is a ranged effect drawn inside its layer's box, over what that layer paints. The usual carrier is a frame-size rectangle with no fill: add_layer {layerType:"rectangle", properties:{x:0, y:0, the scene's width and height, fillEnabled:false, strokeWidth:0, blendMode}, animations:[{effect, parameters}]}, blendMode "add" for light (embers, sparks, dust, bokeh, light-leaks brighten what is below, clipped at white), "normal" for things that cover (snow, rain, fog). A new layer goes on top; move_layers {layers, position:"below", referenceId:"title"} puts it behind a title. It covers the whole scene unless given start/duration; then it shows only in that window (fade it out with from, as sparks-shower does). Several generators can share one carrier.

Settings (the catalog below has every range and default; fog and light-leaks draw soft blobs, the others dots):
- count: most at once (a spark draws up to four shapes). size: a dot's core diameter or a blob's width.
- direction 0 is up, 90 right, 180 down; spread: degrees the launch direction varies; gravity pulls down; wind pushes sideways (+ right); turbulence: how far each one wanders.
- lifetime varies a little per life. embers and sparks age from color to color2; the others pick between them. softness 0 is hard, 1 soft; glow adds a halo; trail: seconds of streak.
- emitter: area fills the box; bottom, top, left, right enter from that edge; point sends them out from originX, originY (fractions of the box).
- prewarm true starts full, false empty; seed picks another arrangement; loop repeats the picture exactly every loop seconds (0 is off).

Time: the picture at time t depends only on the settings, the seed and t, so scrubbing, look, the dashboard, part of a render and a whole export show the same frame at any common frame rate (12 to 60 fps). Animating with from: count, spread and the origin are read when each particle is born (lowering count lets living ones finish, so none pops out); speed, direction, wind and gravity are summed over each life (paths curve smoothly); size, turbulence, colours, opacity, softness, glow, flicker and trail change at once; a from that changes lifetime, seed, loop, emitter or prewarm is refused. A loop repeats exactly while nothing but color, color2, opacity, softness and flicker is animated; lifetimes are rounded so whole lives fit it.

Flashing: embers twinkle up to 7 times a second, sparks 14, the others 3 or less. check scans one second of a generator and reports flashing when big, bright twinkling particles cover much of the frame: lower flicker, size or count.

Cost: the dashboard plays each at 30 fps, even near its caps. A 1080p video frame (mp4, webm, mov) with dots, fog or light-leaks takes about 0.1 s (Chromium's pixels go straight to FFmpeg), so a 6 s 1080p video takes about 20 s. An APNG takes about 0.5 s a 1080p frame (its encoder is slower). A PNG still, PNG sequence or the default (gifski) GIF keeps Chromium's PNG files, which take about 0.3 s a 1080p frame with fog or light-leaks. Fewer, bigger particles are cheaper.

Exports: video, GIF, APNG and PNG draw what the preview shows. Lottie stores a generator as embedded frames, each distinct frame once, so give it a loop (4 s at 30 fps is 120 pictures); it refuses a generator on the add blend (Lottie would draw it as normal) and frames over 64 million pixels, and says what to change.

Nine recipes, each the ops of one edit: library {topic:"rules", name:"particle-recipes"} (add search:"<name>" for one): embers-behind-title, snow-over-footage, sparks-shower (a burst at 1 s that dies out by 3 s), dust-in-light, rolling-fog, light-leak, rain-storm, bokeh-behind-title, snow-loop (repeats every 4 s).

## box-styles: Corner radius and strokes on media and groups
ops: update_layer, update_layers

Images, videos (including their poster frames), and GIF images support uniform radius or independent cornerRadii [top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left], plus stroke paint, strokeWidth, and inside/center/outside strokeAlign. ## shadow: Drop shadows and Shadow animations
ops: add_layer, update_layer, add_animation, update_animation

A drop shadow (a layer's shadow, and the from.shadow and to.shadow values of a Shadow animation) is null (no shadow) or {color as hex (#rgb, #rgba, #rrggbb, or #rrggbbaa, whose alpha is the shadow's opacity), x and y offset in pixels from -1000000 to 1000000 (x to the right, y down), blur in pixels from 0 to 10000}; anything else is refused. add_animation with preset "shadow" adds a Shadow animation (every layer type except audio, which has no shadow and refuses any shadow animation): from.shadow is the layer's current shadow and to.shadow doubles its x, y, and blur with the same color (stopping at those limits), or is {color "#000000", x 0, y 4, blur 12} when the layer has no shadow. update_animation can set either end to any shadow or null. Between from and to, the color mixes red, green, blue, and alpha in a straight line like a Color animation, and x, y, and blur each move in a straight line. A null end acts as the other end's shadow with alpha 0, so the shadow fades in or out in place; two null ends draw no shadow.

Stacked shadows: a layer's optional shadows is a list of up to 32 {color, x?, y?, blur? (each 0 when left out), opacity? 0..1 (1)}, drawn after the single shadow, later ones over earlier ones, on any drawn layer. Long shadow: hard copies stepping one way (x and y 2, 4, 6 ...). Neon: one color at no offset with blur 4, 16, 40. Soft plus hard: a wide faint one, then a tight dark one. The list does not animate (a Shadow animation moves only shadow); [] or null removes it. Lottie export refuses it.

## text-animation: Text animation units, anchors, clipping and Custom text
ops: add_animation, update_animation

Ordinary timed text animations accept textAnchor (layer, letters, words, lines) and textClip (boolean), also as add_animation overrides. Null removes either field. add_animation also takes unit, stagger, order, smoothing and smoothingIntensity as overrides on a text layer (per-unit delays go inside `animation`, below) (a word-by-word reveal is one op: preset "preset-custom-text", unit "words", stagger 0.27); on any other layer they are refused. Anchor controls the center used for rotation/scale independently of Apply to/unit. Text clips use each timing unit's shaped glyph/advance bounds and line-height cell in its resting position, following other non-clipped animations. These settings are refused on non-text, Hide, Show, Change text and Counter parents. Explicit text settings opt into independent per-operation units; older unit-only documents keep their first-unit-mode behavior. Text catalog presets created now carry preset.text:true and textAnchor:layer. Their relative travel uses the design line height (fontSize*lineHeight); previous saved endpoint-only presets retain their values. preset-custom-text creates a 0.5-second In effect with letters, 0.13-second delay, Slow down, and Move Y 100%. Its nullable parameters are moveX/moveY (-1000..1000 percent of line height), angle (-360000..360000 degrees), scale (0..1000 percent of rest), opacity (0..100 percent of rest), color (hex), and blur (0..1000 added pixels). Null disables a parameter; at least one must remain enabled. Text Mask uses movement plus per-unit clipping, with travel distance in line-height percent; older Mask presets retain whole-layer reveal behavior. Edit preset parameters/mode to regenerate values; direct endpoint edits detach preset metadata.

## smoothing: Text unit delay smoothing
ops: add_animation, update_animation

Timed animations accept optional smoothing and smoothingIntensity, also as add_animation overrides. Smoothing distributes text-unit start delays independently from the easing of each unit: smooth, natural, expo-out, quart-out, in-out, ease-out (Slow down), ease-in (Accelerate), linear (None), or four custom Bezier controls. Missing or null smoothing uses linear; null removes the field. smoothingIntensity is an integer 0..100 (default 50), independent of intensity; null removes it. For n units, delay spread is stagger*(n-1), and unit i starts at that spread times the smoothing curve at i/(n-1), clamped to 0..1. order: forward (default), reverse, center-out (the middle unit first, then outward; spread stagger*floor((n-1)/2)), edges-in (both ends first) or random (a seeded shuffle; orderSeed, a whole number 0..4294967295, default 1, only with random). Change text and Counter effects take the same orders. Zero/one unit has no delay. Layer-wide and non-text animations have no unit delay. delays (inside `animation`, not at the top level of add_animation) is a list of seconds after start, one per unit (0 to 60 each, up to 2000; the last one holds for further units), that replaces stagger, order and smoothing: words timed to speech (caption-words karaoke and pop). It needs a unit other than layer; null removes it. scatter {x?, y?, rotation?, scale?, seed?} offsets each unit's away end (from; to on an out) by seeded amounts up to those pixels/degrees and a 1 +/- scale factor; needs a unit other than layer. range (also a top-level add_animation field) times every unit of one animation in place of stagger, like a range selector (engine/textTiming.js): "typewriter", "center-out" or "random" ({preset:"random", seed}) on an in or out, "wave" (each unit to `to` and back as it passes) on a custom; or {shape: square|ramp-up|ramp-down|triangle|round|smooth, start, end, offset (fractions of the text, a number or [from, to] swept by the easing), smoothness, easeHigh, easeLow, amount, order: forward|reverse|random, seed}, or {shape: "wiggly", wiggles (over the animation, default 4), correlation (0 to 1, default 0.5; 1 moves every unit together), seed, amount} (EffectCraft's Wiggly selector: each unit wanders from -1 to 1 on smooth seeded noise, so past from and to; custom animations only), or a list of up to 4 combined by mode (add, subtract, intersect, min, max, difference). An in or out range must take every unit from 0 to 1. Change text and Counter out/in effects accept the other settings and range (a unit other than layer; it times their units over the effect's duration); their parent operations and Hide/Show refuse them.

## from-current: Starting from the current value
ops: add_animation, update_animation

Custom operations without preset metadata accept fromCurrent:true, also as an add_animation override: earlier enabled operations are sampled at this operation's staggered start, in start/list order. Those raw property values replace From during playback; stored From values are remembered and return when fromCurrent is false/null (removed). Existing omitted flags and catalog defaults retain exact legacy behavior, including Letter spacing and Line height's optional From representation. In/Out presets, Composed containers, Hide/Show, Change text and Counter refuse the flag.

## keys: Multi-key animations
ops: add_animation, update_animation

A custom animation may take keys: 3 to 64 of {t, value, ease?} in place of from and to. t is a share 0..1 of the duration, rising key by key; every value names the same properties (any animatable one, effectPreset and rotateX/rotateY too); ease (an easing name or four Bezier numbers) shapes the step to the next key and defaults to the animation's easing. Before the first key the first value holds, after the last key the last. from and to are saved as the first and last value. keys can't be used with motionPath, scatter, range, fromCurrent, preset or discrete; null removes them. add_animation also takes keys at the top level ({layer, keys, start, duration}); keyframes [{time, x, ...}] in scene seconds are read as keys.

## shadow-channels: Animating only some shadow properties
ops: add_animation, update_animation

A timed Shadow animation accepts optional shadowChannels, also as an add_animation override or update_animation property: a nonempty array of distinct color, opacity, x, y, blur strings. Omitted/null (null removes it) or all five keeps the whole-shadow behavior. A partial selection changes only those properties over earlier animations: color changes RGB while keeping alpha, opacity changes alpha while keeping RGB, and each offset/blur changes only its number. Each operation keeps its own start, duration, easing, units, and order. Same-channel overlaps use start time then list order; a whole-shadow operation replaces all channels. An absent current shadow starts as transparent black with x0/y4/blur12; geometry-only operations stay invisible until opacity is supplied. A null endpoint uses the other endpoint's RGB/geometry and zero alpha. Two null endpoints keep current RGB/geometry, clear selected opacity, and retain null when current is absent. Non-shadow, Hide, Show, Change text, Counter and audio operations refuse channel selections. `look` and the PNG, GIF, video, and PNG sequence exports draw the same shadow at every time; Lottie exports a Drop Shadow effect whose color, opacity, direction, distance, and softness are keyed on every frame, with the direction kept within half a turn of the frame before so it never spins the long way.

## resize: Resize animations and anchors
ops: add_animation, update_animation, add_scene, update_scene, insert_component, add_layer, update_layer, update_animations, delete_animation

add_animation with preset "resize" adds a Resize animation: from is the layer's current width and height, to is 1.5 times them (half when that would pass 65536), and anchor is "center". anchor (optional, on an animation whose from or to has width or height) is the point of the box that stays in place on the canvas while the size changes: "center", "top-left", "top", "top-right", "right", "bottom-right", "bottom", "bottom-left", or "left". x and y then move with the size so that point keeps its place, also on a rotated, scaled, or skewed layer (the rotation, scale, and skew at the animation's start are used), and Move animations still move the box from there. Several Resize animations add up. Without an anchor (left out, or null in update_animation, which removes it) x and y stay put, so the box grows to the right and down. Any other anchor value, an anchor on an animation that changes neither width nor height, and an anchor on a Hide or Show are refused. A width or height animation is refused on groups (resizing a group resizes the layers inside it, which an animation does not do), audio, and component instances (use Scale), on text with textResize "width-height" (it sizes itself), and for the height of text with textResize "height" (only its width can change; its height follows its lines, and the anchor counts the height the rewrapped lines really take, so a bottom or center anchor stays put as the text grows taller), whether it comes from add_animation, add_layer, update_layer, insert_component, or the layers of add_scene and update_scene. A size animation saved before these rules (for example a clip-content group's width wipe) still loads and plays: update_animation, update_animations, and update_layer (with the animation under the same id in animations) can still change its name, timing, and easing, or delete_animation remove it; only an edit that changes its width, height, or anchor is refused. `look` and every export (PNG, GIF, video, PNG sequence, and Lottie, whose position and size are keyed on every frame) draw the same box at every time.

## letter-spacing: Letter spacing and its animation
ops: add_animation, update_animation, update_layer

A text layer's letterSpacing is a percentage of its fontSize, from -10000000 to 10000000: 10 on 48 px text adds 4.8 px after each letter, and a negative value pulls the letters together. Documents saved before document version 2 stored pixels; they are converted when read, so every letter keeps its place. add_animation with preset "letter-spacing" adds a Letter spacing animation (text layers only): to.letterSpacing is the layer's letterSpacing plus 20, and from has no letterSpacing. from.letterSpacing is the optional Initial value: without it the animation starts from the letter spacing the layer has when it starts (its own letterSpacing, or where an earlier Letter spacing animation left it); update_animation with from {letterSpacing: n} sets one and with from {} removes it. letterSpacing in from or to is refused on every layer type except text, whatever operation adds it. The text is laid out again on every frame: text with textResize "width-height" or "height" resizes with the spacing, and with unit letters, words, or lines each unit moves with the spacing (stagger and order stagger it like any text animation). `look` and every export (PNG, GIF, video, PNG sequence, and Lottie, whose glyph outlines are keyed on every frame) draw the same letters at every time.

## tilt: 3D tilt (2.5D perspective)
ops: add_layer, update_layer, add_animation, update_animation

Any drawn layer (best a group holding a product screen) can lie on a plane turned in 3D: rotateX and rotateY in degrees (-89 to 89) and perspective, the eye's distance in px (100 to 100000, default 1600; smaller is a stronger perspective). It follows CSS perspective(p) rotateX(rx) rotateY(ry) around the layer's middle: positive rotateX tips the top away, negative rotateY brings the right side closer. The layer's own rotation, scale and skew happen on the plane first. Set them as properties (update_layer {layer:"Screen", properties:{rotateX:32, rotateY:-22}}) or animate them: add_animation {layer:"Screen", preset:"tilt", start:0, duration:4, easing:"smooth", from:{rotateX:40, rotateY:-28, perspective:1400}, to:{rotateX:28, rotateY:-16, perspective:1800}} is a slow camera drift and push. Without values, preset "tilt" tips a flat layer to rotateX 30, rotateY -20 (or lays a tilted one flat) over 1.2 s. The drawing stays vector-sharp: it is cut into a grid of triangles, each drawn with its own flat transform, within 0.3 px of the true perspective. Lottie export refuses a tilted layer (use video, GIF or PNG); a live (unframed) video inside a tilted group does not play in the app preview.

## anchor: Pivot point and skew
ops: add_layer, update_layer, add_animation

anchor [x, y] is the point rotation, scale and skew turn about, in percent of the layer's box (0 0 top-left, 50 50 the center and the default, 100 100 bottom-right; -1000 to 1000); null puts it back at the center. It moves only the pivot: an unrotated, unscaled layer stays where it is. add_animation's top-level anchor ([x, y] or a name like "top-left", "bottom") on a rotate, scale, grow, shrink or skew animation sets the layer's anchor (on a Resize it is the Resize anchor; an anchor inside animation or in update_animation is only a Resize's): a door swing is add_animation {layer:"Card", preset:"rotate", anchor:"left", from:{rotation:-90}, to:{rotation:0}}. skewX and skewY (degrees, -89.999 to 89.999; skewY absent is 0) slant the layer and animate like any value: from:{skewX:20}, to:{skewX:0}. Lottie keeps both (its anchor is the layer's anchor).

## repeater: copies of a shape, path, text or group
ops: add_layer, update_layer, add_animation, update_animation

One layer drawn as several copies: a ring of dots, radial rays, a row of bars, a burst. repeatCopies 1 to 64 (the original counts; 1 is off), and each copy moves repeatX, repeatY px, turns repeatRotation degrees and scales by repeatScale (1 = same) from the copy before, so the steps compound. repeatAnchorX, repeatAnchorY: the point copies turn and scale around, px from the layer's middle (0 0). repeatStartOpacity and repeatEndOpacity (0 to 1) fade the first to the last copy. Twelve rays around a point: add_layer {layerType:"rectangle", name:"Ray", properties:{x:470, y:95, width:18, height:110, radius:9, repeatCopies:12, repeatRotation:30, repeatAnchorY:120}}. Every field animates; a fraction of a copy fades the last one in, so add_animation {layer:"Ray", preset:"repeat", start:0, duration:0.8} grows the copies one after another (on a layer without a repeater it makes 8 copies 45 degrees apart). The layer's own move, effects and mask apply to all copies. Lottie export refuses a repeater (use video, GIF or PNG).

## shape-modifiers: Zig zag, offset path, twist, pucker/bloat, round corners
ops: add_layer, update_layer, add_animation

Rectangles, ellipses, stars and paths change their outline before fill and stroke. zigzagSize (px, -1000 to 1000, 0 off) pushes points off the outline, zigzagCount teeth per side (1 to 100, default 5; an ellipse has 4 sides), zigzagPoints "corner" (default) or "smooth" (a wave). offsetAmount (px, -1000 to 1000, 0 off) grows the outline, or shrinks it below 0; offsetJoin "miter" (default), "round" or "bevel" shapes the grown corners. roundCorners (px, 0 to 1000) rounds every sharp corner, say a star's tips or a path's joins. bloatAmount (-1 to 1, 0 off) moves points toward the middle and bows the sides out (a star becomes a flower); below 0 it puckers them into spikes. twistAngle (degrees, -3600 to 3600, 0 off) turns the outline around twistX, twistY (px from the middle): the farthest point turns the whole angle, nearer points less. Order: round corners, bloat, twist, zig zag, offset (teeth stay per side after a twist; past about 3000 points sides take fewer teeth). A stamp edge: add_layer {layerType:"ellipse", properties:{width:300, height:300, zigzagSize:10, zigzagCount:6}}. The numbers animate: from:{offsetAmount:0}, to:{offsetAmount:20}; a spinning swirl: from:{twistAngle:0}, to:{twistAngle:180}. Lottie gets the outline as drawn.

## text-loop: Text loops (wave, float, pulse, jitter)
ops: add_layer, update_layer

textLoop (text layers only; null removes it) keeps each unit moving between the layer's in and out animations, stacked on them: {style: wave (units rise and fall in sequence) | float (each bobs and sways on its own seeded pace) | pulse (each grows about its own center) | jitter (each jumps to a seeded spot and tilt), amount? (px, 0..1000; pulse: percent it grows; jitter also tilts amount/2 degrees), speed? (cycles a second, jitter: jumps a second; 0.01..60), stagger? (seconds each unit lags the last, 0..10), unit? (letters default | words | lines), seed? (0..4294967295, default 1), ramp? (seconds it eases in and out, 0..10, default 0.3)}. Defaults: wave 12 px, 1/s, 0.08 s; float 8 px, 0.5/s, 0; pulse 12%, 1/s, 0.08 s; jitter 3 px, 8/s, 0. Per unit it runs from the end of its latest in animation (from the start without one) to the start of its earliest out (to the end without one). Lottie bakes it per frame.

## line-height: Line height and its animation
ops: add_animation, update_animation, update_layer

A text layer's lineHeight is a multiple of its fontSize, from 0.01 to 100 (1.2 on 40 px text puts the lines 48 px apart); a Line height animation uses the same number. add_animation with preset "line-height" adds a Line height animation (text layers only): to.lineHeight is the layer's lineHeight plus 0.4 (at most 100), and from has no lineHeight. from.lineHeight is the optional Initial value: without it the animation starts from the line height the layer has when it starts (its own lineHeight, or where an earlier Line height animation left it); update_animation with from {lineHeight: n} sets one and with from {} removes it. lineHeight in from or to is refused on every layer type except text, whatever operation adds it, and must be 0.01 to 100. The text is laid out again on every frame with the animated line height, so every line moves: text with textResize "height" or "width-height" grows or shrinks its height (keeping its top, middle, or bottom in place by its verticalAlign), a fixed box keeps its size and places the taller or shorter block by its verticalAlign, and with unit lines (or letters or words) each unit moves with its own line height (stagger and order stagger it like any text animation). A Line height and a Letter spacing animation can run on the same text layer, one after the other or at the same time, and each frame is laid out with both values. `look` and every export (PNG, GIF, video, PNG sequence, and Lottie, whose glyph outlines are keyed on every frame) draw the same lines at every time.

## hide-show: Hide and Show operations
ops: add_animation, update_animation, add_scene, update_scene, insert_component, add_layer, update_layer, set_layer_flags, update_animations

add_animation (sceneId, layerId, and either preset with an optional start in seconds, or a full animation object) also adds the instant Hide and Show operations: preset "hide" or "show", or animation {name, kind: "hide" or "show", start}. Hide and Show are a point in time with no duration, easing, from, to, unit, stagger, order, or anchor (those fields are refused, also when update_animation changes a timed animation's kind to hide or show). A Hide at start hides the layer from that time until a later Show, and a Show shows it from that time on. Several apply in time order, operations at the same time apply in list order, and the state at the operation's own time is already the new one. Before its first operation a layer keeps its own visibility, except that a layer whose first operation is a Show is hidden until that Show. A layer hidden with set_layer_flags (visible false) stays hidden at all times. A layer hidden by an operation also hides the layers inside it. A mask layer hidden by an operation masks nothing while it is hidden, like a mask hidden with set_layer_flags (visible false): the layers it masks show whole. A video layer's sound is silent while an operation hides the video or a group around it, in video exports. start must be from 0 to the scene's duration in add_animation, add_layer, insert_component, and add_scene, and in update_animation, update_animations, update_layer, and update_scene (with layers) when they give a Hide or Show a new start (or turn a timed animation into one), so a Hide or Show left past the end of a scene that was made shorter can still be renamed or switched between hide and show. `look` and every export (PNG, GIF, video, PNG sequence, and Lottie, where the opacity switches with hold keyframes) show the same frames.

## change-text: Change text operations
ops: add_animation, update_animation

add_animation with preset "change-text" (text layers only, with an optional start) adds a Change text operation, or give a full animation {name, kind: "change-text", start, text, overlap, out, in}. text is the new string (the same limits as a text layer's text), overlap is true or false (default false), and out and in are each null (no effect) or a text effect {preset, duration, easing, unit, stagger, order, orderSeed, distance}. out.preset is "fade-out", "shrink-out", or "blur-out"; in.preset is "fade-in", "slide-up", "slide-down", "slide-left", "slide-right", "grow-in", "blur-in", or "rotate-in". An effect's duration is 0.001 to 600 seconds (default 0.6), easing is any animation easing (default "smooth"), unit is "layer", "letters", "words", or "lines" (default "layer"), stagger is 0 to 60 seconds between units (default 0), order is "forward" (default), "reverse", "center-out", "edges-in" or "random" (orderSeed 0 to 4294967295, default 1, only with random), and only a Slide has distance (pixels, 0 to 1000000, default 80). Unknown effect settings are refused. The preset adds the layer's own text as the new text (ready to change with update_animation), overlap false, a Fade Out and a Fade In of 0.6 seconds each. A Change text has no duration, from, to, unit, stagger, order, or anchor of its own (those fields are refused); its start must be from 0 to the scene's duration when it is added or given a new start, and start plus its effects (Out then In, or the longer one with overlap) may not pass 600 seconds. update_animation sets text, overlap, out, or in; a new out or in replaces the whole effect, so give every setting you want to keep (left-out settings take their defaults). At start the string shown until then plays the Out effect and disappears when its last unit has finished; the new string appears when the Out effect ends, or at start when overlap is true (both strings are then drawn while the effects run), plays the In effect, and stays. With out and in both null the text changes instantly: the state at exactly start is already the new text. Several Change text operations on a layer chain in time order (same start: list order), and one turned off with enabled false is skipped. The layer's other animations (Move, Fade, Color, letter-by-letter text animations, and so on) apply to whichever strings are drawn. Text with textResize "width" or "width-height" sizes its box to the string drawn; while two strings overlap each has its own box, kept at the layer's alignment anchor. The layer's own text property stays the text before the first Change text. `look` and every export draw the same strings; Lottie exports each string as its own layers whose in and out points are the frames that string is drawn.

## counter: Counter animations
ops: add_animation, update_animation

add_animation with preset "counter" (text layers whose text has a number, with an optional start) adds a Counter, or give a full animation {name, kind: "counter", start, duration, easing, from, to, step, out, in}. A layer that isn't text, or a text without digits, is refused with the reason (for example "A Counter needs a number in the text, such as 0, 42%, or $1,234.50; “Hello” has no digits."). An existing Counter can still be edited after its text lost its number; it then shows plain numbers. from and to are numbers from -1000000000000 to 1000000000000, step is a number from 0.0000000001 to 1000000000000 (it always counts toward to, so it is never negative), and from to to is at most 1000000000000000 steps. duration is 0.001 to 600 seconds, start is 0 or later, start plus duration may not pass 600 seconds, and easing is any animation easing. The preset counts from the number in the text to 100 more (100 less near the largest value), by 1, for 1 second with "smooth" easing, without effects. out and in are each null (no effect) or a text effect with the same settings as a Change text's. At every step the old number plays the Out effect and the new number the In effect, both from the moment of the step; an effect that would last longer than the time until the next step is made shorter (its duration and stagger in proportion), and the last step's effects play in full. A Counter has no unit, stagger, order, anchor, text, or overlap of its own (those fields are refused). update_animation sets from, to, step, start, duration, easing, out, or in; a new out or in replaces the whole effect. Before start the layer shows its own text. From start to start plus duration it shows from plus the eased progress of the way to to, rounded down to whole steps (toward from), and at the end exactly to, even when the distance isn't a whole number of steps (0 to 10 by 3 counts 0, 3, 6, 9, 10). It counts up or down. Every number keeps the format of the number in the layer's own text: its prefix and suffix (such as $, kg, or %), its sign, its zero padding (0000 shows 0042 and grows to 10000), its thousands separator (a comma, a dot, or a space; a dot is otherwise the decimal point), and as many decimals as the most precise of the text, from, to, and step. The layer's own text property stays unchanged. Change text and Counter operations on one layer run in time order; the next one takes over the number shown at its start. `look` and every export draw the same numbers. Lottie exports each run of frames that shows one number as its own layers, shown only on those frames (in and out points); when the In and Out effects would need more than 3000 text layers, the numbers are exported without them and the export warns with lottie-counter-effects-simplified.

## media-play: Timed media (Play) on video, audio and GIF layers
ops: add_layer, update_layer

Timed media (Play) on video, audio, and GIF image layers uses these layer properties, all in seconds: mediaStart (scene time where Play begins), mediaDuration (how long Play lasts; 0 means until the source ends for video and audio, and no Play for an image), mediaOffset (time inside the source file where Play starts reading), mediaFreeze ("none", "first", "last", or "both": whether the first frame shows before Play and the last frame stays after it; otherwise the layer is hidden outside Play), and mediaSourceDuration (the source file's length: the duration in seconds that importing it answers, from project {action:"import"} or import_media; video and audio Play stops there even when mediaDuration is longer). A GIF image plays only when mediaDuration is greater than 0; it then loops by its own frame delays for the whole Play range. Without Play a GIF shows its first frame. A video past its end holds its last frame. Video and audio layers also have volume, from 0 (silent) to 1 (the file as recorded, the default); a Volume animation changes it over time, and duck_audio turns music down under a voice-over; lowpass (Hz) muffles the sound (topic sound). To play the file faster, slower, backwards or choppy, see topic clip-speed.

A video layer's log ("apple-log", or null for none, the default) converts log footage, which looks flat and gray as recorded, to Rec.709: the Apple Log curve per channel, a highlight roll-off, then BT.2020 to BT.709 color. Importing a video tagged BT.2020 with no transfer curve (how Apple Log is tagged) or with a tag naming Apple Log says so and puts log:"apple-log" in its add_layer example. replace_media sets log from the new file's import; a cut_out subject keeps the clip's log, a silhouette or matte has none.

## clip-speed: Clip speed, slow motion, reverse and choppy frames
ops: add_layer, update_layer, update_layers, add_animation

Video, audio and GIF image layers can play their file faster, slower or backwards. These optional layer properties are set with add_layer or update_layer (null removes one; a layer without them plays its file at normal speed, forwards, every frame):
- mediaSpeed (video, audio, GIF; 0.05 to 16): a constant speed. 2 plays twice as fast, 0.5 at half speed.
- mediaReverse (video, audio, GIF; true or false): plays the same stretch of the file backwards, from its end back to mediaOffset. It needs a known end: mediaDuration, or for video and audio mediaSourceDuration (the length importing the file answered).
- mediaFrameRate (video, GIF; 1 to 120): shows a new picture only this many times a second, for a choppy, stop-motion look. The sound is not stepped.
- mediaInterpolation (video; "nearest" (default), "blend" or "flow"): how a render draws a moment between two file frames, which a slowed-down clip asks for. nearest shows the file frame on screen at that moment; blend mixes the two frames around it (halfway shows 50% of each); flow first makes in-between frames with FFmpeg's motion interpolation (slow: about 5 to 13 frames a second on a fast Mac; made once per file and kept). flow first drops the file's repeated frames, so a 30 fps screen recording whose picture changes only a few times a second plays smoothly at any speed, with each new picture at its own time. flow keeps a sudden change (a cut) as a cut, and a video with a see-through picture is blended instead (the render's warnings say so). The preview and the dashboard show nearest.
- mediaPitch (video, audio; "keep" (default) or "follow"): at a constant mediaSpeed, keep plays the sound at its own pitch (FFmpeg atempo) and follow makes it higher when faster and lower when slower, like a tape. A Speed animation always plays the sound tape-style, so its pitch follows the speed whatever mediaPitch says. mediaReverse plays the sound backwards too.

A Speed animation changes the speed over time: add_animation {preset: "speed"} (from the layer's speed down to a quarter of it), or a full animation {kind: "custom", start, duration, easing, from: {speed}, to: {speed}} with speeds 0 to 16 (0 pauses the file). As with every animation it keeps its to value after it ends, until a later one starts. The file moves on by the area under the speed curve (added up in steps of 1 ms), so the preview, every render, the sound and the dashboard all play the same moment of the file. mediaStart and mediaDuration stay on the scene's clock (mediaDuration 2 at 0.5× plays 1 s of the file) and mediaOffset is a time in the file; mediaDuration 0 plays until the file ends, which takes longer when slower. Slow motion in the middle of a clip: two animations, {kind: "custom", start: 1, duration: 0.5, easing: "smooth", from: {speed: 1}, to: {speed: 0.25}} and {kind: "custom", start: 2.5, duration: 0.5, easing: "smooth", from: {speed: 0.25}, to: {speed: 1}}.

The sound of a clip with a speed is rendered on the scene's clock before the mix, so mp4, webm and mov renders and the dashboard's playback hear the same thing and the mix is exactly the scene's length. A final render with motion blur still shows one file frame per output frame; a blend or flow clip shows the mix of the frame's own time, the same picture as look and a render without motion blur. `check` does not count a Speed animation as a move. add_captions with layerId, detect_beats and retime follow the speed (topics captions and beats).

## sound: Volume, fades and ducking
ops: duck_audio, add_animation, update_animation

Video and audio layers have volume, from 0 (silent) through 1 (the file as recorded, the default) to 4 (+12 dB, for a quiet recording; the dashboard preview plays at most 1, renders play the full gain). A Volume animation changes it over time: add_animation with a full animation whose from and to hold volume, such as a fade in {kind: "in", start: 0, duration: 1, from: {volume: 0}, to: {volume: 1}} or a fade out {kind: "out", start: 5, duration: 1, from: {volume: 1}, to: {volume: 0}}. It takes what other animations take: easing, intensity, a start such as "cue:drop" or "after:title.in", update_animation, and enabled false. A volume outside 0 to 4 is refused, and so is a Volume animation on any layer but video and audio (no other layer has sound). As with every animation, the one that started last sets the value, and an In animation holds its From until it starts. update_layer or update_layers volume on a layer whose Volume animations go to or from its old volume scales them all by new / old, so fades and dips keep their shape at the new level (receipt movedWithLayer). Playback through a view, comment or edit link, and mp4, webm and mov renders, follow the volume over time: the render reads it 1000 times a second and multiplies the sound by that smooth curve (no steps at video frames), and the master limiter and the loudness numbers measure the faded mix. A layer without a Volume animation is mixed exactly as before. A clip with a speed or played backwards plays its sound that way too, keeping its pitch or not (mediaPitch, topic clip-speed).

A linear Volume fade from or to 0 is equal power (sin in, cos out), so two sounds crossfading keep their level instead of dipping 3 dB in the middle; other easings keep their curve.

A spoken line (a layer at volume 0.5 or more whose file has a transcript) is cleaned on render, then leveled to -16 LUFS as cleaned (each line on its own, gain changing mid-pause, when one clip's lines, split at pauses over 0.6 s, need gains more than 6 dB apart): a 90 Hz rumble cut, a de-esser, a gentle compressor (3:1, threshold 7.5 dB over the line's own loudness, so only peaks lose about 3 dB; no make-up gain) and a voice EQ (a balanced voice preset at half strength). voiceCleanup false on a video or audio layer turns the cleanup off (a voice already mixed); null removes it.

Video and audio layers can also have lowpass, a low-pass filter's cutoff in Hz (20 to 20000; 0, null or none is off): sound above it is turned down, so a low cutoff sounds muffled, and 20000 is fully open. A Lowpass animation moves it, for music muffled until the drop: {kind: "in", start: 6, duration: 2, easing: "ease-in", from: {lowpass: 400}, to: {lowpass: 20000}} (a drop at 8 s; an In holds 400 before it starts). The cutoff moves evenly in octaves (halfway from 400 to 20000 is about 2800 Hz), as the ear hears it. Left out of from, a Lowpass animation starts from the layer's lowpass, or 20000 when it has none. Renders, the dashboard's playback and view links apply it (FFmpeg's 2-pole lowpass, the cutoff updated every 5 ms; a Web Audio filter in view links). Like Volume animations, it is not a motion for the motion checks.

duck_audio {sceneId, layerId, under, to?, fade?, fadeUp?} turns one audio or video layer (the music) down while other audio or video layers play over it (under: their names or IDs, such as a voice-over). Each stretch in which an under layer plays (its Play range, without the times a Hide silences a video) gets a dip (a transcribed voice dips for its words; a measured sound without words, such as a sound effect, dips only while it is over -44 LUFS, and the report's loud says so); an under layer with neither mediaSourceDuration nor mediaDuration counts as playing to the scene's end, so set mediaSourceDuration to the duration importing its file answered. In a dip the volume goes down to to (0 to 1) in the fade seconds (0 to 10, default 0.15) before the stretch starts, stays there, and comes back up in the fadeUp seconds (default 0.8) after it ends, to the volume the layer would have then. Without to, kino measures the files' loudness (LUFS) and sets the music 9 LU under the voice (a transcribed voice counts at the level the render levels it to: -16 LUFS, or off it when leveling's 12 dB limit leaves a very quiet or loud voice there; an automatic dip never raises the music above its level there), and the receipt's depth says so; unmeasured, to is 0.25. A transcribed voice dips for its words; the music comes back up in pauses over 1.2 s. A dip that covers a section cue (named intro, verse, chorus, build, drop, break, bridge or outro) adds a warning: the change is heard muffled. Stretches that overlap, or are so close that the fades would meet, become one dip, and fades are cut short at the scene's start and end. Each dip is two Volume animations named "Duck down" and "Duck up" (easing smooth). Running duck_audio again on the layer first removes the ones it made, so run it again after moving a voice-over. A Volume animation that starts inside a dip takes over from it, so start a fade out after the last dip or from the dip's volume. The receipt's duck gives {layer, to, dips, summary, times: [[start, end], …]} (when one edit ducks several layers, duck is a list of these, one per layer): each dip from the start of its fade down to the end of its fade up (at most 20; more counts the rest), and a note naming under layers whose length is unknown. summary says the same in words with the under layers each dip covers, such as "music ducked to 0.3 at 0.2-7.4 s (line-a, line-c) and 10.9-14.9 s (line-b)" (four dips, then "and N more").

## voice: Separating dialogue from background
ops: isolate_voice

isolate_voice {sceneId, layerId, keep?, engine?, amount?} splits the sound of an audio or video layer into its voice and everything else (music, noise, room sound), on this computer: nothing is uploaded. It reads the layer's src as the operations before it in the same edit left it, and the file must be in kino's media library. keep "voice" (default) makes the layer play only the voice, "background" only the rest, and "both" makes it play the voice and adds an audio layer named "<layer name>-background" (without a file extension) right beside it, with the same mediaStart, mediaDuration, mediaOffset, volume and Volume animations, so later operations in the edit can turn either one down by name. The layer keeps its ID, name, animations, Play and volume: its src points at a new library file, "<file> (voice).m4a" for sound, or for a video "<file> (voice).mp4" or ".webm" with the same picture (copied, not encoded again) and the new sound. A video's sound still stops while it is hidden; the background layer has no Hide/Show, and the receipt says when that matters. Saving, history revert, renders and the dashboard mix use the new files like any import, and voice plus background add up to the original sound.

engine "auto" (default) uses the best engine this computer has: "dpdfnet" (a 10.6 MB model downloaded once from github.com into the data folder's models folder, then offline; about 11 dB cleaner speech on kino's test mix), "apple" (macOS 13 and later; about 9 dB) or "rnnoise" (FFmpeg, everywhere; about 4 dB, mostly hiss and noise). amount (0 to 1, default 1) is how much background to remove; 0.5 leaves half. Results are kept: the same file with the same engine and amount answers at once with the same library files. The receipt's voice gives {engine, seconds, cached, keep, note?}; note says why auto skipped an engine. A refusal names its voiceError (VOICE_MODEL_UNAVAILABLE, VOICE_ENGINE_UNAVAILABLE, VOICE_ENGINE_FAILED, VOICE_NO_SOUND) and says what to send instead. KINO_VOICE_DOWNLOAD=0 turns the model download off.

## speech: Cleaning a voiceover: transcript, cuts and captions
ops: cut_speech, add_captions, place_broll, keep_line

inspect {targets:["speech:<layer>"]} transcribes an audio or video layer (at speed 1) on this computer with whisper.cpp and answers its words as index:word start, in scene seconds, about 100 characters a line, with ‖seconds before a word after a pause over 0.6 s. Above them a flags line names the slips by index: repeat (a word or phrase said twice in a row; the first saying is flagged), filler (um, uh, er, hmm), maybe (like: sometimes meant), unclear (sound with no word heard, or a short word whisper wrote 4 or more times in a row on noise; shown as (unclear)), low (words the model was unsure of: check the spelling) and pause. A long transcript is paged: the last line says "N more from Xs: inspect {targets:["speech:<layer>[from=X]"]}". The words keep their indices after cuts (a cut word no longer shows), so read them once and cut by index. The first inspect of a file takes about a sixth of its length (61 s of speech in about 10 s on an M-series Mac); the transcript is then kept beside the library file (<file>.words-<model>, plus -<hash> when the project has names) and answers at once, with "saved transcript" in the header. whisper is told the project's names (name-like words of the brief and style spec, in alphabetical order), and a sentence of a file over 8 s with a word under 50% confidence or unclear sound is heard again alone; the surer reading is kept when it shares most of the words. The header lists the names and a re-heard line per such sentence (file seconds, both readings, which was kept). A voice cut_speech cut keeps the transcript its cuts were made on (its cut indices point into it), also when the brief's names change; the header then says so, and cut_speech {layer, restore:"all"} lets it be heard with the new names. Word starts were within 0.1 s of hand-marked times for 85% of words (mean 0.05 s) on kino's test voiceovers.

cut_speech {sceneId, layerId, words? | spans?, pauses?, restore?, ripple?} cuts words (indices) or spans [{from, to}] (scene seconds) out of the voice. Each cut runs from the quiet point before a word to the quiet point before the next word kept, so no breath or half-word is left. The layer becomes pieces: the first keeps its ID and name, the others are "<name>-2", "<name>-3" … (same src, same group), each playing the next stretch of the file right after the one before, with 12 ms Volume fades ("Join in", "Join out") at the joins so there is no click. pauses (0.05 to 10 seconds) also shortens every pause between kept words that is longer than that, from its middle. Or name a pace instead of a number (silence removal): "natural" shrinks pauses over 0.6 s to 0.4 s, "tight" pauses over 0.35 s to 0.25 s, "fast" pauses over 0.2 s to 0.12 s; half of what is kept stays beside each word as padding, and shorter pauses stay as spoken. ripple true (default) moves everything after each cut earlier by the time removed (other layers' animations and Play, cues, rhythm, background animations and the scene's length) as retime does; ripple false closes up only the voice and leaves a gap at the end. Run it again on a cut voice with more indices (the pieces are rebuilt). The receipt's speech gives {layer, removed (seconds), words, pauses, pieces, scene ("12s → 11.1s"), moved (what moved earlier, by how much from when)}. Add captions after cutting: captions made before a cut no longer match the voice (the receipt says so).

Morph cut: a cut on a video voice (a talking person on camera) hides the jump in the picture. Each piece after the first gets morphIn (seconds, default 0.3; cut_speech morph sets it, morph false sets 0 for plain cuts; update_layer morphIn 0 to 1 changes one join). Over that window, centered on the join, the render shows optical-flow in-betweens from the last frame before the cut to the first after it (engine/morphCut.js; the flow is worked out at 640 px wide at most and scaled up). A join whose two frames look like two different shots stays a plain cut, with the render warning morph-cut-skipped. The flow is render only: the live preview, check and look frames draw a cross-dissolve over the same window instead (the outgoing piece holds its last frame, the incoming one its first), so they cost nothing extra; the render skips the dissolve for a two-shot join that stays a plain cut. A 0.3 s morph adds about 7 s to a 1080p render per join (0.6 s for the flow, 0.7 s per in-between frame on an M-series Mac). The receipt's speech.morph says how many joins morph.

restore puts cut words back: cut_speech {layer, restore:[indices]} (the "cut" line of inspect speech:<layer> lists the cut indices) or restore:"all" (every cut, back to one piece), alone in its op (no words, spans or pauses). Each word comes back from the quiet point before it to the quiet point before the next word, where it was cut; pieces that play on from each other become one again, keeping the first piece's ID and name, and their Join fades go. ripple true moves everything after each place later by the time put back; ripple false moves only the voice's later pieces; left out, restore uses the ripple of the voice's last cut, so it moves back exactly what that cut moved. Any piece's name works as layer. Animations a later piece had of its own (a Duck) stay on the joined piece. restore:"all" also puts back pauses that pauses shortened (the cut line says "pauses only" when no word was cut). An index before the first or after the last word the voice plays (cut there, or trimmed off by Play) extends the voice to it. The receipt's speech gives {layer, restored (seconds), words, pieces, scene, moved}. A word that already plays is refused.

inspect speech on a cut voice: the first piece ("<name>") lists the whole voice and a "pieces" line names them; a later piece ("<name>-2") lists only the words it plays, and its header says which piece it is.

add_captions {speech:"<layer>", fix?} captions the words the voice plays now (topic captions).

place_broll {speech:"<voice layer>", clips:{"<video layer>":"<a few words>"}, lead?} times B-roll to the spoken concept. Each clip goes on the spoken word where most of its words (small and common words left out; "cities" meets "city") are said within 6 words, the earliest on a tie, never inside another clip's stretch. It comes in lead seconds (default 0.2, 0 to 1) before that word and stays to the end of the sentence (the word ending in . ? or !) plus 0.15 s, on through later sentences that say one of its words again, at least 1 s, and never past the next clip's start. kino sets the clip's mediaStart, mediaDuration and mediaFreeze "none" (hidden outside it), and mediaOffset to the start of the first of the file's own shots (found at import) long enough for it, one frame past the cut, not one another clip of the same file already uses. The receipt's broll lists each clip placed (times, the word and its index, the shot) and each clip none of whose words is said (left as it was). Put the clips above the voice's picture; cut_speech first, then place_broll, since a later cut moves the clips with everything else.

keep_line {layer, line? | from?, to?, text?, subtitle?, look?, name?, duck?, dip?} keeps one spoken line of a clip's own sound. line is its words as said (found in the clip's transcript, case and punctuation ignored, fillers skipped); or from and to are scene seconds (no transcript needed, for speech whisper cannot read), and text gives the subtitle's words instead of the transcript's (a fix or a translation), shown over the whole line. The clip's sound plays only the line (at the layer's own volume; a layer at volume 0 is set to 1), with short fades ("Line up", "Line mute", "Line down", Volume animations); another keep_line on the layer keeps both lines. The subtitle is add_captions with look (default "subtitle"), named line-at-<seconds>s (name changes it; subtitle:false adds none). The music layer (duck: its name; default the audio layer named music or song, else the only other audio layer; "none": no dip) dips to dip (0 to 1, default 0.5, about 6 dB) under the kept stretches as duck_audio does and replaces its earlier dips. For a clip with music under its speech, send isolate_voice {layer} in the same edit after keep_line: keep_line reads the transcript of the sound you heard, then isolate_voice swaps the file for its voice-only one and keeps the Line animations and volume. kino names a clip's saved lines once when every layer that has them is silent (status or an edit's notes, with this call ready); the song alone stays a choice. A line the layer plays only part of, misses by 1 s or less, or plays at another speed is named as "plays 5.3-7.4, line at 7.1-9.2" (file seconds); at speed 1 its call starts with update_layer moving mediaOffset (and mediaDuration for a longer line) to play it whole. A line shortened with "…" is kept by from and to. keep_line reads the transcript those lines came from, also one heard before the brief's names changed. The receipt's keepLine gives {layer, line, scene: [start, end], file: [start, end] (seconds in the video), volume, kept (stretches), subtitle: {name, pages, look} or "off" or "none", duck: {layer, to, summary} or "none", summary, notes?}.

The first transcript needs whisper-cli (macOS: brew install whisper-cpp) and a model file such as ggml-small.en.bin in ~/.kino/models/ (or KINO_WHISPER_MODEL set to its path; KINO_WHISPER_CLI names another binary). kino downloads nothing: without them the refusal says what to install. The audio is never sent anywhere.

## cutout: Subject cut-out
ops: cut_out

cut_out {sceneId, layerId, output?, engine?, grow?, feather?, color?, invert?, points?, exclude?, time?, above?, name?} finds the subject of a video or image layer (the person, animal or object in front; several subjects count as one) in every frame, on this computer: nothing is uploaded. It reads the layer's src as the operations before it in the same edit left it, and the file must be in kino's media library (an SVG or an animated GIF is refused). It adds a new layer: a copy of the layer (as duplicate_layers makes it: same place, size, turn, group, opacity, Play settings, clip speed and animations, so it stays exactly over the layer in every frame) whose src points at a new library file, without the layer's blur and shadow and without sound (the layer keeps playing it). The copy is named name (default "<layer name>-<output>", so later operations in the edit can use it) and goes right above the layer or, with above (a layer name or ID; referenceId is the same), right above that layer, moving into its group and keeping its place on the canvas as move_layers does.

output "subject" (default) is the subject on a transparent background: "<file> (subject).webm" (VP9 with transparency) for a video, ".png" for an image. A title between the layer and its subject cut-out looks as if it were behind the subject; a blurred or replaced layer under it is a new background. "silhouette" is the subject's shape filled with color (default "#ffffff"; a colour name or #rrggbb), transparent elsewhere: grown and put under a subject cut-out it is an outline, feathered it is a glow. "matte" is the subject white on black ("<file> (matte).mp4" or ".png"), opaque, for create_mask: a layer masked by it shows only inside the subject, and with invert true (black subject on white) only outside it. grow (-100 to 100, in the layer's pixels at scale 1, default 0) makes the shape larger or, below 0, smaller; feather (0 to 200, default 0) softens its edge over about that many pixels. Subject and silhouette layers get allowOverlap, because check measures layer boxes and a cut-out is transparent around its subject (contrast then reads a title behind it against what is behind the title): use look to see that it still reads.

engine "auto" (default) uses "subject" (Apple Vision's subject mask: any foreground subject, macOS 14 and later) or, on macOS 13, "person" (people only). Both are part of macOS (nothing is downloaded); on other systems cut_out is refused with CUTOUT_ENGINE_UNAVAILABLE. The matte is steadied over 5 frames so its edge does not flicker. A frame with no subject is empty there (the receipt's note counts them); when no frame has one the operation is refused (CUTOUT_NO_SUBJECT). A cut-out takes about 1.5 seconds per second of 1080p video, and cut-outs are made one at a time. An edit waits up to 50 seconds in all, however many cut_outs it has; a longer one keeps going in the background and the operation is refused with CUTOUT_RUNNING and how far it got: nothing was changed, so send the same edit again later. Results are kept by the file's contents: the same file and engine reuse the subject found before (cached true), and the same settings reuse the same library file, which answers at once even while another cut-out is being made. The receipt's cutout gives {engine, output, frames, seconds, cached, note?}. A refusal names its cutoutError (CUTOUT_ENGINE_UNAVAILABLE, CUTOUT_NO_SUBJECT, CUTOUT_NO_PICTURE, CUTOUT_ENGINE_FAILED, CUTOUT_RUNNING) and says what to send instead. A video's cut-out has the clip's frame rate (a variable one is made constant) and 8-bit colour.

points picks objects: 1 to 8 [x, y] in % of the clip's own picture (look {mode:"source"}), kept with exclude (the same, dropped) at time (seconds into the file, default 0). Only the subject engine's objects under points are kept (a point on none takes the nearest within 5% of the picture; an exclude point drops only the object right under it), and each later and earlier frame keeps the objects that overlap most with the ones kept in the nearest frame; without points every object is kept, as before. points need the subject engine (macOS 14); the receipt adds picked {frame, objects}, and for a clip lost and weak: [[from, to]] file seconds where nothing was kept, or the kept area fell under half or grew past 1.5 times the last sound frame, or jumped over half its size (not judged while cut by the picture's edge), with seeAt: the scene seconds of each span's first frame (look {mode:"frame", t}). No object under the points is CUTOUT_NO_SUBJECT.

## update-layers: Editing several layers at once
ops: update_layers, update_layer

update_layers (sceneId, layerIds, properties) changes several layers in one operation. properties holds stored values (opacity 0 to 1, times in seconds). Each listed layer takes only the properties it has and the others keep theirs: for example fontSize changes only text layers, width and height skip component instances (they take their main component's size) and audio, volume changes only video and audio, and Play settings change only layers that already have Play (give a GIF Play with update_layer). Unlike update_layer, a group's new width or height resizes the layers inside it, text boxes refit to their new text, size, or font and pick up the new font file, and Play settings are kept inside each layer's own source (a mediaDuration of 0 is kept). update_layers refuses id, type, parentId, componentSceneId, src, and animations, a property none of the listed layers has, and a Play setting that is not a number.

## update-animations: Editing several animations at once
ops: update_animations, update_animation

update_animations (sceneId, animationIds, properties) changes several animations at once: every listed animation gets the same properties, exactly as update_animation would give one, for example {start: 1} or {duration: 0.5, easing: "linear"}. Animation IDs are unique in a project, so animationIds lists 1 to 5000 animation IDs, or {layerId, animationId} pairs that also check which layer each animation is on, and the animations may be on different layers of the scene. patch is accepted in place of properties. It refuses an empty or missing list, an animation that is not found (or not on the given layer), an animation listed twice, both properties and patch, empty properties, and id. Each animation is checked the way update_animation checks one (for example a Hide or Show refuses a duration, and no animation may end after 600 seconds), and the whole call is refused when one of them fails. To move several animations by the same amount, or give each its own new values, use one update_animation per animation in the same call.

## enabled: Turning an animation off
ops: update_animation, update_animations

Any animation, a Hide or Show included, can be turned off for playback: update_animation or update_animations with {enabled: false} turns it off, and {enabled: true} turns it on again; any other enabled value is refused. A turned-off animation stays in the document (inspect shows it with enabled false; an animation that is on has no enabled field) and can still be moved, resized, copied, staggered, and deleted, but `look` and every export (PNG, GIF, video, PNG sequence, and Lottie) skip it: it changes no values, a later animation starts from the values it would have without it, a turned-off Hide or Show does not change visibility, a turned-off text animation by letters, words, or lines does not split the text, and it does not count toward a component's play length.

## stagger: Staggering animations
ops: stagger_animations

stagger_animations (sceneId, items, delay, optional order, optional seed) staggers animations or layers: it changes only start times, so consecutive items start delay seconds apart. items lists 2 to 5000 entries, each an animation ID, an {animationId} or {layerId, animationId} pair (one animation), or {layerId} alone: that layer's animations, and those of the layers inside it when it is a group, which move together as one block and keep their timing relative to each other. The animations may be on different layers, and animation and layer entries may be mixed. delay is a number of seconds from 0 to 600 (0 lines every start up). The first item starts where the earliest of all the listed animations starts now, and every animation keeps its duration. order is "timeline" (the default: the timeline's rows, top row first, which is the scene's layers array order with each layer's animations in their own order; an item's row is its first animation's row), "reverse" (bottom row first), "selection" (the order of items), "center" (the middle of the timeline order first, then outward on both sides; items equally far from the middle start together, so two items from the center both keep the earliest start), or "random" (a shuffle of the timeline order; seed, a whole number from 0 to 4294967295 that defaults to 1, picks the shuffle, and the same seed always gives the same order; seed is refused with other orders). It refuses a list with fewer than 2 entries, an animation or layer that is not found (or an animation not on the given layer), a layer with no animations, an animation listed twice (also through a group and its layer), an unknown order, and a delay that would make any item end after the scene's duration (a text animation by letters, words or lines ends when its last unit has played, as playback measures it): nothing is shortened or squashed, and the refusal names the longest delay that fits. Hide and Show operations move with their item as points in time. `look` and every export use the new start times.

## component-overrides: Component instances and overrides
ops: set_component_overrides, reset_component_overrides, detach_component, insert_component

A component instance (a layer of type "component") draws its main component scene (isComponent true) and follows every change to it. Its componentOverrides change some properties of the layers it draws without changing the main component: {"<componentPath>": {property: value}}. A component path is source layer IDs from the main component scene joined with "/": "title" is the main component's layer title, and "card/photo" is the layer photo inside the main component of the nested instance card. Every ID before the last must be a component instance; groups are not part of a path, and a path names at most 17 layers. Every layer type can override name, visible, x, y, rotation, opacity, blendMode, blur, and shadow; rectangle, ellipse, star, path, text, and group layers also fill and fillEnabled; these layers plus images and videos support stroke, strokeWidth, and strokeAlign; rectangle, group, image, and video layers radius and cornerRadii; groups clipContent; rectangle, ellipse, star, image, and video layers width and height; text layers text, fontFamily, fontSource, fontSize, fontWeight, fontStyle, lineHeight, letterSpacing, textAlign, verticalAlign, and textCase; image layers src, and mediaSourceDuration together with src; video and audio layers volume. Nothing else can be overridden (IDs, type, parentId, animations, timing and Play settings, masks, non-group clipContent, textResize, scale, skew, and the size of groups, paths, and nested instances). Properties an instance doesn't override keep following the main component. An x or y override moves the layer's position animations by the same amount; other overrides change only the resting value. Auto-sized text refits to an overridden text or font and keeps its alignment anchor. An overridden picture keeps the main component's box, and a GIF plays only when the main component's layer has Play. Overrides of an outer instance win over the overrides a nested instance has in its own main component. set_component_overrides (sceneId, layerId of an instance, overrides, optional replace) merges each entry into the instance's entry for that path, property by property, or with replace true makes overrides the instance's whole set. It refuses a path that leads to no layer of the main component, a property that layer can't override, and a value the layer can't have (for example opacity 2); a value equal to what the main component shows is dropped, except an explicit mediaSourceDuration (including zero) stays with a differing src so later base-source edits cannot change the replacement's length. reset_component_overrides (sceneId, layerId, optional componentPaths) removes the listed paths and every deeper path under them, or every override without componentPaths. When the main component changes, overrides of layers that no longer exist are dropped. detach_component bakes the overrides into the detached layers. `look` and every export draw the instances with their overrides.

## quality-settings: What the checker needs to know: roles, video type, assertions, waivers
ops: update_project, waive_check, unwaive_check, add_layer, update_layer, update_layers, update_scene

The `check` tool judges a video by what each layer is for. These fields never change how anything draws.

- A layer's `role` is headline, subhead, body, caption, cta, label (small UI text on a plate or tag, such as a callout's; never taken for the headline, so it needs only the body size), logo, hero, decor, background or ui (the product's own readable text: UI labels, rows, small captions in its words; kept in the contrast check, skipped by type_min, reading_time and hold, never the headline). Set it with add_layer or update_layer `properties.role`; a role one letter off (headlin) is corrected and listed in `fixed`. Without one, the checker guesses: the largest text is the headline, other text is body. decor and background are skipped by the text size and overlap checks.
- `perpetual: true` on a layer marks motion that runs the whole time on purpose (a drifting background), so it is not flagged as distracting or linear. A slow push or drift needs no flag: a custom animation (not an entrance or exit) of 2 s or more that changes only position and scale, on a layer that is not text and fills at least half the frame (a plate, the world group) or has role background, and whose part on screen travels under 4% of the frame's diagonal a second on average (scaleX and scaleY 1 to 1.12 over 3 s is about 2%), may be linear and longer than the hero band. A text entrance or a small layer's drift is judged as any move.
- `allowOverlap: true` on a layer lets it cover or touch other text on purpose. The contrast gate then reads a text drawn below it against what is behind the text, not against this layer.
- update_project `videoType` (promo, ad, clip, explainer, sting, other) sets the length and pacing checks. promo_basics (logo and message by 5 s, a hero covering 30% of the frame, a CTA in the last 20%) runs only for promo and ad.
- update_scene `properties.assertions` lists up to 100 promises the scene must keep, checked by the assertions gate: `{type: "visible_by", target, time}` (the layer is fully shown by `time` seconds), `{type: "before", target, other}` (the layer appears before `other`), `{type: "inside_frame", target}` (it never leaves the frame while shown). Each may have a `note` of up to 200 characters. An empty list removes them.
- update_project `rhythm: {bpm, offset}` gives the music beat in beats a minute, starting `offset` seconds in. With it, `check` compares big motion with the beat.
- waive_check {code, target, reason} accepts a finding on purpose: a layer, a scene, or * for every finding of that rule (the what-viewers-see, crop, shape and face-cut rules take named layers only: a glob that reaches every layer counts as *; a whole-scene finding (text_slides, ending_seen, held_static) is kept in the person's words by naming the scene). target may be a list (["arch-1","s2"]) or a glob ("arch-*", "walk-?"), saved as one waiver per layer it matches when the edit runs: a layer added later is not covered, so waive it again. A component copy is waived by its instance layer. It needs a reason. A waived finding stays listed as waived and no longer blocks a final render. unwaive_check {code, target} removes the waiver. An edit that takes one rule past 5 waivers says so in its notes. A waiver of a rule that no longer fires on the current revision (stale) counts nowhere: a full check that measured everything (no transcript, people search or media facts missing) lists the stale ones in one line, with the unwaive_check that drops one.
- request_unmet compares the edit with what the person asked for: the brief lines in their words and their notes. Words in quotes must be on screen; music, a voice, captions, a logo, sound effects and an end card must be there ("no logo", "remove the music" the other way round). A miss holds the final render (waive_check request_unmet when the person changed their mind); a note with none of these is listed to confirm by looking. restraint warns on a transition away from a shot change, two color grades on one layer at once, one moment of a footage file shown twice and letterboxed footage.
- reference_range (a full check, 2+ saved references measured) warns when the edit's median brightness, dark share, text entrances a 10 s fall outside theirs, or no clip's own speech plays before the loudest song change (fit_music's song- cues, else the music's saved song shape) while most of theirs have a voice there (sung or spoken: whisper hears both). The fix lifts the dark stretch with a color-wheels gamma, or names the lines your clips say (keep_line), else the footage finder; or waive_check {code:"reference_range", target:"<scene>", reason} keeps it. look versus says it in one line.
- look {mode:"versus"} rows put like next to like: your opening beside theirs, your loudest change (the heaviest song cue) beside theirs (heard), your ending beside theirs, the rest at the same point of the story; a moment of the same kind (app, type, footage, end card) wins at that place, else another kind with no gap numbers. A board move.ref pin fills its own shot's row; one reference still fills at most 2 rows while another can. Under a pair, "theirs on screen" gives the reference's labels there (type, overlay, composite, color, made, frame, spoken, plain and words) when it has them.
- `look {mode:"motion", ref:"sound"}` ends with the scene-cut list and the mix check, measured from the previewed mix (never played): a spoken line under 6 LU over the music, a true peak over 0 dBTP before the limiter, a jump of 10 LU at a clip's edge, 1 s of silence inside the video, and a loudness range over 8 LU (phone speakers).
- `check {fix:"auto"}` applies the fixes marked (auto) as one edit; history can undo it. `library {topic:"rules", name:"quality"}` lists every rule with its limit.
- A face the crop cuts (crop_no_subject, an error) and a narrow window (narrow_window) offer `frame_shot {layer}`: kino's own crop on the mark or found face, or the whole shot over a blurred copy (the file's size and faces come from kino). fix:"auto" applies it only for a cut face (crop_no_subject) on a layer nothing moves, never for narrow_window (no face was found; look first); a cut that is meant: look at its source, then mark_subject the part (a waiver on a layer nobody looked at is refused: look_first). A waive_check on crop_no_subject, head_cut_off or narrow_window that is refused answers with next: edit {ops:[frame_shot {layers}]} for the layers it named (mark_subject for one an animation moves); another refused code answers with check {fix:"auto"} when its finding has an auto fix, else the finding's own fix op, else check {rules:[code]} (its words). The mark-misses-a-face warning (crop_no_subject) comes only when the crop slices that face; a face wholly outside the window gives none.
- A waive_check on "*" (or a glob that covers every layer) of a per-layer rule is allowed; the edit receipt's notes say "waive_check <code> covers N layers". A final render's refusal and receipt end with the waived count ("39 waived (crop_no_subject 17, ...)"), stale waivers left out.

## cues: Named moments: cues
ops: set_cues, add_captions, detect_beats

`document.cues` is one list of named moments, [{name, time, kind}], sorted by time. Times are seconds on the clock of the scene they belong to (the scene with the music or the captions). Names are 1 to 64 letters, digits, `.`, `_` or `-`, starting with a letter or digit; at most 5,000 cues. Kinds: marker (placed by you), beat and onset (found in music by detect_beats), line and word (from captions or a transcript, by add_captions). `inspect {targets:["cues"]}` (or `"cues[kind=beat]"`) lists them with their times and the rhythm; start animations at those times. `"cues[from=60,to=90]"` (also with kind=, as `"cues[kind=beat,from=60]"`) lists only the cues in that stretch of seconds. One answer lists the earliest cues that fit about 10,000 characters; when more are left its last line says how many and the target that lists the next ones. The receipt says what detect_beats and add_captions made. A cue {name, time, kind, layer, media} is pinned to footage: media is seconds into that video or audio layer's file, and time is worked out again from the clip's mediaStart, mediaOffset, mediaSpeed and mediaReverse each time the project is read (set_cue {name, layer, media} makes one; a clip with a Speed animation is refused; a deleted clip leaves a plain cue). After split_layer or cut_speech the cue moves to the piece of the same file that shows its moment; inspect cues says "not shown" when none does.

set_cues {cues: [{name, time, kind?}], replace?, remove?} adds cues; a cue with the name of an existing one replaces it. replace true clears every cue first, replace "beat" (or another kind) clears only that kind. remove: ["name", …] deletes those cues (an unknown name is refused). A list left empty removes the field.

## captions: Captions from SRT, WebVTT, a transcript or plain text
ops: add_captions

add_captions {sceneId, …} makes readable captions in a scene. Give one source:
- text: the text of an SRT or WebVTT file (found by its first line; or set format "srt" | "vtt"), or plain text with format "text" (or text that is neither). Plain text is shown from start to end (default the whole scene), each page for its reading time; text that cannot be read in that span is refused.
- file: an absolute path to a local .srt, .vtt or .txt file (at most 1 MB). The kino command line and the MCP tools read it and send its text; nothing leaves the machine.
- words: a timed transcript [{text, start, end}] in seconds, one word each (from your own speech-to-text or a script). kino sends no sound anywhere.
- speech: a voice layer (audio or video at speed 1). kino transcribes it on this computer (topic speech) and captions the words the scene plays, after any cut_speech, without um/uh or unclear sound. fix {"Acmes":"Acme", "help him":"help Acme"} replaces misheard words or runs of words (no case or punctuation needed; "" leaves them out); the receipt's captions.speech counts the words and fixes, and notFound lists fix keys that matched nothing.

Times in the file are seconds on the scene's clock plus offset (offset -107 shows a cue at 1:47 at 0 s). With layerId (a video or audio layer of the scene) they are seconds into that layer's file instead, such as a transcript of the clip: each time is placed where the scene plays that moment of the file (after its mediaStart, from its mediaOffset, at its speed, so at 0.5× a caption shows twice as long), and pages outside the part of the file the layer plays are cut or dropped. Give offset or layerId, not both; layerId is refused for plain text and for a layer played backwards. Pages outside the scene are dropped and pages crossing its edges are cut (the receipt counts both). timing "source" (default) keeps the file's times and only trims overlaps; "fit" also lengthens pages too short to read (up to the next page and 7 s) and closes gaps under half a second.

Paging follows common English subtitle rules: at most 42 characters a line and two lines, one line when it fits, lines broken after punctuation and never after a weak word (a, the, of …); a page needs 20 characters a second (the reading_time check uses the same rate), lasts 5/6 s to 7 s, and has at least 2 frames before the next. Longer events are split into pages that share the event's time. Each line must also fit the frame's safe area at fontSize.

What it makes: a group `name` (default "captions") holding one group per page (captions-1, captions-2, …) that draws the box and is shown from the page's start to its end with Show and Hide; inside, the text `captions-1-text` (role caption, centered). Cues: line cues captions-1, … at each page's start, and word cues captions-w1-hello, … when the source has word times (VTT inline times or words). Running add_captions again with the same name replaces that group and its cues; delete_layer on the group removes the captions.

Style: fontSize (default 48 on a 1080-pixel frame, scaled to the frame), fontFamily (Inter), fontWeight (600), fill (#FFFFFF), box (a hex color, default #000000B8, black at 72%; false for none, then check contrast), position bottom (default, just above the bottom safe edge) | middle | top, appear cut (default; best for reduced motion) | fade (in 0.3 s, out 0.2 s, shorter when a page has little time to spare beyond characters ÷ 20; a page with under 0.1 s to spare cuts. The field is `appear` because `animation` names an animation). The receipt's `captions` gives pages, events, split events, cut or dropped pages, and pages that are too fast, short, long or close together.

Looks: `look` picks a named caption look; its own fields (fontSize, fill, box, position …) still win. Sizes are shares of the frame height, capped by its width. subtitle (the pages above on a dark box) and editorial (Noto Serif pages, no box, soft shadow, fade) keep the paging above. The phrase looks show the spoken words as they are said, so the page limits do not apply: clean (3-word phrases, Inter, white with a soft shadow, rising in), pop (3-word phrases revealed word by word; the key word 18% bigger in `accent`, default #FFD84D, popping in; `keywords: ["word"]` names them, else the longest word that is not a small word), word (one word at a time, big and centered), highlight (3-word phrases; the word being said takes `accent`), bold (2-word phrases in dark type on a light card, sliding up, with a hook title). A phrase never spans a pause of 0.35 s or a sentence or clause end, and is hidden in silences (it leaves 0.3 s after its last word unless the next starts within 0.6 s). Every phrase passes reading_time: one said too fast to read once it has faded in takes the next phrase in (up to twice the look's words, 3 for word, never across a sentence end) when holding it would put the captions over 0.3 s behind the voice, else it holds and the next waits; a word revealed as it is said shows early enough to be read. Portrait frames take a third line before a phrase is made smaller. The first phrase and one after a silence enter 40% slower. A hook stays at least the headline's smallest size (type_min) and gives up its last words to fit 3 lines. `reduced: true`, or a compose scene made with reduced, fades the phrases with no rise, pop or slide; highlight colors the word on the text itself. `hook: true` (bold's default) shows the first sentence (at most 7 words) big in the upper third while it is said, and the captions start after it; `hook: "<text>"` shows that text for the first 2.5 s. The receipt adds look, phrases, hiddenInSilences and hook.

An mp4, webm or mov render of a scene with captions, played forward (normal playback), also writes .srt and .vtt files with the pages' times next to the video, cut at options.duration when the render is shorter than the scene. Stills, GIF, APNG, Lottie and reversed or boomerang renders get no caption files. Blank lines inside a page are left out of those files, and "-->" in the text is written "->" in the .srt.

## sync: Syncing two clips of the same moment
ops: sync_clips

sync_clips {sceneId, layerId, toId, search?, at?} (layer and to: two video or audio layers of one scene, at speed 1) makes layer play in step with to: the same moment of both files plays at the same scene time. It changes only layer's mediaOffset, and mediaStart when layer's file would have to start before it (the layer then starts where its file does); to stays where it is. Without at it matches the files' sound on this computer (FFmpeg decodes the first 10 minutes; how much new sound starts every 10 ms, in 24 bands from 150 Hz to 6 kHz, slid past each other to the best fit) and handles a short clip inside a long one. Shifts where less than half the shorter file (or 2 s) overlaps are not counted. search (seconds) looks only within that far of how the clips line up now. confidence is 1 − (the best other fit more than 0.25 s away ÷ the best fit); below 0.4 (two different moments, a steady beat, near silence, or a file with no sound track) nothing changes and the refusal gives the best guess and says to ask the person for a sync point or find one with look {mode:"source"}. at {layer, to} gives the seconds into each file that show one moment, and no sound is needed. The receipt's sync gives {layer, to, by: "sound" | "at", confidence, overlap, same: "<seconds> s of <layer> = <seconds> s of <to>", mediaStart, mediaOffset, inStep (the scene seconds where both play) or note}. The curves are kept beside each file (.sync), so syncing several clips to one long recording decodes it once. On kino's first real test, a phone filming a walk with an app beside the app's 115 s screen recording, the right clip matched at confidence 0.62, 1.04 s apart (the app's own beeps found in the clip and frames side by side agree), and five clips of other moments scored 0.16 or less.

## beats: Finding the beat in music
ops: detect_beats

detect_beats {sceneId, layerId, onsets?, rhythm?} analyses the sound of an audio or video layer on this machine (FFmpeg decodes it; the first 10 minutes are read) and writes cues: beat-1, beat-2, … on each beat (kind beat) and onset-1, … on each sound that starts (kind onset; the 1,000 strongest at most; onsets:false leaves them out). Each onset has weight (0 to 1: how hard it hits against the song's hardest hits, so a hi-hat or a soft intro weighs little) and band (low: kick and bass, mid: snare, clap, voice, high: hi-hats); the beats that start a bar have downbeat:true; accent-1, … (kind accent, with weight, band and rank, 1 = the section's hardest) are the hardest hits of each section (fit_music's song-* cues, else 8-bar phrases): at most one per bar, about one per 4 bars, none under weight 0.3. Earlier beat, onset and accent cues are replaced. Each beat is placed by the sound within 4 s of it, but the tempo and where the beats start come from the whole file: two versions of a song with the same opening give the same first beats (within about 1 ms) when the rest keeps the same tempo and beat, or is louder, shorter or silent; when the rest changes tempo or puts its hits between the opening's beats, the first beats can move or be missing, so check firstBeat and cuesMoved after replacing the sound. Animations keep their times: when this run moves or removes the beat cue an animation starts on (or its onset cue, when no beat was there), the receipt's `beats.cuesMoved` lists it as {animation, cue, at (its start), moved (seconds) or gone, now? (the cue at its time now, a beat first, else an onset)}; move it with update_animation properties {start:"cue:<name>"} or leave it. It analyses the layer's src as the operations before it in the same edit left it, so a sound set by an earlier update_layer is the one read. A beat right at the start of the sound (0 s) is found too; hiss, room tone or music already playing when the file starts is not taken for one. Times are on the scene's clock: the layer's Play start, offset and speed are applied (at 0.5× the beats come half as often on the scene), and beats the layer does not play are left out. With mediaSpeed the bpm and rhythm are the tempo the scene hears (120 at 2× is 240); a Speed animation changes the tempo as it plays, so detect_beats sets no rhythm for that layer and the receipt's note says so. When `retime` (a building block) moves the layer's Play start, the beat and onset cues and the rhythm move with it and cues it no longer plays are removed; when the cues could be from more than one sound, or the layer now plays more of its file, the receipt's notes say to run detect_beats again. With a steady beat (confidence 0.5 or more) it also sets the project rhythm {bpm, offset: first beat}, which `check` compares motion to; rhythm:false keeps the rhythm as it is. The receipt's `beats` gives bpm, confidence (the share of beats with a sound on them), the counts (accents as "3 (top per section)"), the first and last beat, and a note when the beat is uncertain or missing (silence, a steady tone, under two beats). The result is kept beside the file, so the same sound is analysed once (a result kept by an older version is made again). Tempo range 60–200 a minute; on test click tracks the beats land within a millisecond.

## fit-music: Music as the edit's structure
ops: fit_music

fit_music {sceneId, layerId, payoff?, turn?, end?, plan?, drop?, recut?} measures the song of an audio layer (its beat analysis and its sections: intro, build, drop, break, outro; both kept beside the file) and plays one unbroken stretch of it: the stretch whose drop lands exactly on payoff (default: the spine's payoff beat start). Nothing in the song is skipped or reordered. Among the song's drops it takes a heavy one with a break or build before it in the stretch, a song change near turn (default: a spine beat with role turn) and a start on a section line. A stretch that starts inside the song fades in to the next bar line (none on a section line); it plays to the song's own ending when that falls at end (default the scene end), else it fades out over 1 to 2 bars (4 s at most) at end. Time the picture to the song, not the song to the picture: the notes say where the nearest song change to the turn lands ("move the turn there") and where the song's 4-bar phrase ends near the end ("end the video there"). There are three plans, picked by the video's story: "stretch" (the default: steady energy), "stop" (a pause before the payoff: the song's opening up to its own stop, then a drop of whole 4-bar groups on the payoff, then the song's last bar; pieces <layer>, <layer>-2, <layer>-3, joined 2 ms before the hit with 3 ms and 5 ms fades, cue song-stop-<n>) and "recut" (several moments of the song); the receipt's plans lists the plans not used, one line each. drop: the song second of the drop to land on the payoff (stop and recut; without it the planner picks one). The song's own ending never runs past end: when it cannot end by then the last piece fades over 1 to 2 bars. The first piece of the video is also left on a whole group after its latest drop, break or build. recut:true is plan "recut": pieces <layer>, <layer>-2, ... in song order, each cut on a measured bar line and joined as stop joins (2 ms before the hit, 3 ms and 5 ms fades, no crossfade), a piece that is left playing whole groups of 4 bars (a piece that cannot play 4 bars is dropped and the notes say so), a drop on the payoff, an earlier drop as near turn as whole 4-bar groups allow (the notes say where it lands), the song's own ending at end. Running it again replaces its pieces. Volume animations on the layer (ducks, fades) are removed: run duck_audio after it. The receipt's music gives {layer, pieces, plays: "1:14-1:54" (the song times played), sections: ["0 s intro", "20 s drop (payoff)", "36.5 s fade"], notes?} in scene seconds, and the sections become marker cues song-<kind>-<n> (an earlier fit's are replaced). Refused when the song has no steady beat or no drop.

Cutting footage to music: compose {footage, music, pace: "promo" | "velocity", accents?} (a building block) cuts the picture shots as runs: one length in whole beats, repeated, changing only on a bar line, every cut on the song's beat grid (from beat indexes, so a run never drifts). promo is about 2 beats a shot with one hero hold (the title's shot) of at most 5.5 s; velocity is 1 beat a shot. The energy arc: the first 4 bars after the title run at half the rate (calm: 4-beat shots in promo), the last 5 to 8 beats before the end card at twice it (at least 1 beat), and the end card starts on a beat. accents:true (off by default: a downbeat alone does not motivate an accent) adds a zoom punch (Accent: scale +6% in 0.08 s, settling in 0.3 s) on every other downbeat in the open and every downbeat in the ending, cut or not, never under spoken words, the title or a designed transition; check's duration_band reads the names Accent (a scale punch of 0.15 s or less) and Accent settles (0.5 s or less) as quick on purpose. Speech shots keep their words whole and the next cut returns to the grid. The plan in words (dry_run) lists the runs, such as "runs 4x4, 6x2, 7x1 beats; 5 downbeat accents".

## sequences: One video from several scenes, with transitions
ops: add_sequence

add_sequence {properties: {id?, name}, scenes: [scene, scene, …], transition?, transitions?, background?} adds a scene that plays the listed scenes (2 to 50, by name or code, all the same size) one after another. Each part is a component instance of its scene, so editing the scene changes the sequence; the listed scenes become main components (`check` then judges them inside the sequence). Render or check the sequence scene to see the whole video.

transition applies to every change of scene; transitions lists one per change (scenes − 1) and overrides it. Each is a name or {type, duration?, direction?, ease?, ...knobs}: cut (default), crossfade, push, wipe, zoom, whip or spin; duration 0 to 5 s (default 0.5); direction left (default) | right | up | down for push and whip (the way the content moves) and wipe (the way the edge moves); ease any easing name or [x1, y1, x2, y2] (default smooth, natural for whip and spin). Knobs: wipe softness 0..100 (default 10) and the wipe settings (shape, angle, centerX, centerY, edgeColor, edgeWidth: topic preset; the last part leaves exactly where the next appears, the band rides on the next; Lottie export refuses a sequence that uses any of them); zoom amount -4..4 (default 1: the last part grows to 110%, the next from 80%; 2 doubles it, negative zooms the other way); whip blur 0..0.5 of the frame (default 0.2; 0 none); spin turn -360..360 degrees (default 90) and blur 0..90 degrees (default 30). A pair in transitions borrows transition's ease, and its knobs when the type matches. whip is a push that snaps (fastest at the middle) under a directional blur along the move, up to a fifth of the frame at the middle; spin turns the last part 90° out and the next in from −90° while they crossfade, under a spin blur up to 30°. Both put an edge fill (mirror) on the parts while they move, so the smear and the turned frame show picture, not the background (library topic blur). A transition overlaps the end of one part with the start of the next, so the sequence lasts the sum of the scenes minus the transitions, at most 600 s (10 minutes). In a crossfade, wipe, zoom or spin both parts change together, so their text is never fully shown at once (no overlap finding). A scene must be at least as long as its two transitions. Put music on the sequence scene itself.

## video-formats: Whole-video formats filled from slots
ops: format

format {name, slots, aspect?, scene?, id?, seed?} builds a whole video in one call: product-launch (16:9, 30 s), app-demo (16:9 or 9:16, one 4.2 s step per screenshot or screen recording; a video plays silent from when its step enters), social-promo (9:16, about 15 s), feature-announcement (16:9, 9:16 or 1:1) and testimonial (1:1, 16:9, 9:16 or 4:5). Each sets the frame, look, feel, beats, beat lengths (a calm logo open, one hero hold of up to 5.5 s, readable holds, a cut-on-motion into the end card at about twice the opening energy), transitions and marker cues per section (open, headline, hero, feature-1, outro, ...). slots are typed: text with a length limit, lists with a count, imported pictures or videos by name, logo or name, music (an imported song, laid under the whole video as <scene>-music, then cut to the beat by fit_music with its drop on the hero, the quote or the headline; a song with no steady beat or no drop stays as laid and the notes say why) and brand (a brand kit). Copy that needs more reading time than a beat's planned length makes that beat and the video longer, and the receipt says by how much. The result is a compose scene: variants, retime and compose {scene} change it after. library {topic:"formats", name} gives a format's slots and an example op.
